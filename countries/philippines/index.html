Philippines: digital government profile

Reviewed 2026-11-14 · UN E-Government Development Index: 73 of 193 · 0.762 · 2024

Lead agency
Department of Information and Communications Technology (DICT), lead implementing agency under the E-Governance Act (RA 12254), runs eGovPH and eGovDX; Philippine Statistics Authority (PSA) runs PhilSys (National ID) and civil registration; Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) oversees payments; National Privacy Commission (NPC) enforces data protection; DICT and the Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center (CICC) handle cyber incidents; DTI (business names, AI Roadmap 2.0), SEC (company registry) and DOST (NAICRI) also have roles
Strategy
E-Governance Act, RA 12254 (signed 5 Sep 2025; implementing rules signed and E-Government Master Plan approved 24 Mar 2026); Konektadong Pinoy (Open Access in Data Transmission) Act, RA 12234 (lapsed into law 24 Aug 2025; implementing rules released 5 Nov 2025); National Cybersecurity Plan 2023–2028 (EO 58, Apr 2024); National AI Strategy Roadmap 2.0 (DTI, Jul 2024); Philippine AI+ Infrastructure Masterplan 2026–2033 (DICT with ADB, launched 8 Sep 2026); digital-payments target of 60–70% of retail transactions for 2023–2028

Building blocks

AreaWhat existsStatus
Digital identityPhilSys (PSA): 92,016,069 registrations as of 5 Feb 2025; PSA said 91 million Filipinos had registered and been issued national ID numbers by end-Jan 2026. National ID authentications reached 94.7 million by end-Mar 2026 (64 million government, 17.2 million financial accounts, 13.4 million private sector), up from 56,520,952 with 87 relying parties on 17 Sep 2025. Over 92 million Digital National IDs issued through the eGovPH app, with over 300 million eVerify identity checks (Jul 2026)Operational
Digital paymentsBSP: digital payments made up 64.7% of retail payment volume in 2025, up from 57.4% in 2024, within the 2023–2028 target of 60–70%; QR Ph handled 2.47 billion transactions worth ₱1.16 trillion in 2025 and overtook debit and credit cards for the first time. InstaPay: 4.656 billion transactions worth ₱11.554 trillion in 2025; PESONet: 117.2 million transactions worth ₱13.191 trillion. BSP Circular No. 1238 requires interbank transfer fees to be in line with fees for transfers within the same institutionOperational
Data exchangeDICT’s e-Government Data Exchange Platform (eGovDX) linked more than 1,000 government services (Jun 2025) and had processed over 950 million transactions by Jul 2026. RA 12254 provides for a Philippine Government Interoperability Framework, an Integrated Government Network as the main channel for data sharing between agencies, and access to agency data through secure APIs; the implementing rules signed on 24 Mar 2026 require agencies to adopt common interoperability standardsPartial
Civil registration (CRVS)PSA civil registry: 18.73 million transactions in 2025 (18.75 million in 2024); the online share rose from 6% in 2019 to 27–30% after the pandemic. An e-Certificate service launched in Feb 2026 issues QR-coded digital birth, marriage and death certificates (₱290) that can be shared by e-mail, portal upload or APIPartial
Business registryDTI Business Name Registration System: 1.02 million business names registered in 2025 (882,821 new, 137,629 renewals), down nearly 4% from 1.06 million in 2024. The SEC’s online eSPARC system cut company registration from 34 days to 3; a record 52,304 new companies were registered in 2024, with 527,710 active companiesOperational
Government services portaleGovPH super app (DICT, launched 2023): 61 million downloads and over 900 million transactions, linking 1,332 government services across 95 modules (Jul 2026), passing the original target of 30 million users by 2028; some users lost access for up to two days in Apr 2026 during a usage surge. DICT is asking for about ₱2.7 billion for e-government in 2027, about ₱800 million of it for eGovPH. RA 12254 requires a single citizen front-line service delivery platformOperational
Data protectionData Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173), enforced by the National Privacy Commission (NPC); breaches must be notified within 72 hours. NPC Advisory No. 2026-01 says publicly available personal data is still covered by the law and that scraping needs a lawful basis. In Nov 2026 the NPC was reviewing breach notifications from 10 government bodies, including the SEC, BIR, DICT, DTI and DOJ; the SEC incident was detected on 6 Nov 2026. [To verify: status of proposed amendments to the Data Privacy Act]Law or policy
CybersecurityNational Cybersecurity Plan 2023–2028, adopted by Executive Order No. 58 (Apr 2024). On 12 Aug 2026 the House approved HB 9605, the National Cybersecurity and Critical Information Infrastructure Protection Act of 2026, which would create a National Cybersecurity Agency under the Office of the President with fines of up to ₱5 million; it goes to the Senate. After intrusions at government websites, DICT and CICC ordered 24-hour readiness checks for agencies and critical infrastructure (Sep 2026)Law or policy
AI governanceNational AI Strategy Roadmap 2.0 (DTI, launched Jul 2024) with a Center for AI Research. DOST launched the National AI Center for Research and Innovation (NAICRI) on 26 Feb 2026 to anchor the national AI strategy. The Philippine AI+ Infrastructure Masterplan 2026–2033 (DICT with ADB, launched 8 Sep 2026) foresees about US$34.4 billion of investment. A risk-based AI regulation bill was at House technical working group stage in Jul 2026; no AI law has been enactedPartial
ConnectivityKonektadong Pinoy Act (RA 12234) lets data transmission providers register with the NTC instead of needing a congressional franchise and opens up sharing of towers and other passive infrastructure. Free Wi-Fi sites rose from about 4,000 in 2022 to almost 19,000 in 2025. The World Bank Philippines Digital Infrastructure Project (US$287 million, approved Oct 2024) is funding the national fibre backbonePartial
