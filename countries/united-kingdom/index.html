United Kingdom: digital government profile

Reviewed 2026-11-22 · UN E-Government Development Index: 7 of 193 · 0.958 · 2024

Lead agency
Government Digital Service (GDS), part of the Department for Digital, Culture, Media and Sport (DCMS) since the July 2026 machinery-of-government changes that broke up DSIT; DCMS also houses the Office for Digital Identities and Attributes, and Stephanie Peacock MP is Minister for Digital Government. AI strategy, public-sector AI adoption and the AI Security Institute moved to the Cabinet Office (Office for the Prime Minister and Cabinet); the National Cyber Security Centre (part of GCHQ) leads on cyber security
Strategy
A blueprint for modern digital government (Jan 2025) and the cross-government Roadmap for modern digital government (Jan 2026, to 2030), which the Burnham government said it would continue; AI Opportunities Action Plan (Jan 2025; one-year update Jan 2026 reported 38 of 50 actions met)

Civil registrationBirths and deaths in England and Wales are registered locally by registrars (570,988 deaths registered in 2025); statutory medical examiner scrutiny of deaths since 9 Sep 2024; the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 introduces an electronic register for births, still-births and deaths, with consequential fee regulations (SI 2026/672) in force from 9 Nov 2026Operational

Data protectionUK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (Royal Assent 19 Jun 2025; main data protection changes in force 5 Feb 2026); on 30 Sep 2026 the Information Commission, a board-led body corporate, replaced the ICO with the same powers (interim chief executive Paul Arnold; chair recruitment ongoing)Law or policy

Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill, introduced 12 Nov 2025, extends NIS rules to managed service providers and large data centres with 24-hour/72-hour incident reporting; Lords Report Stage concluded 26 Oct 2026 [To verify: Royal Assent]Partial
