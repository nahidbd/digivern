Peru: digital government profile

Reviewed 2026-11-28 · UN E-Government Development Index: 58 of 193 · 0.807 · 2024

Lead agency
Presidencia del Consejo de Ministros (PCM), through its Secretaría de Gobierno y Transformación Digital (SGTD), national technical-normative authority and governing body of the Sistema Nacional de Transformación Digital; it runs gob.pe, the Plataforma de Interoperabilidad del Estado (PIDE), the Centro Nacional de Seguridad Digital (CNSD) and the Centro Nacional de Innovación Digital e Inteligencia Artificial; RENIEC issues national identity documents (DNI, DNIe, digital DNId) and keeps the civil register; Banco Central de Reserva del Perú (BCRP) oversees payments and wallet interoperability; Autoridad Nacional de Protección de Datos Personales (ANPD, Ministry of Justice and Human Rights) enforces data protection; SUNARP runs the company registry
Strategy
Política Nacional de Transformación Digital al 2030 (DS 085-2023-PCM, published 28 Jul 2023, in force to 31 Dec 2030; led and monitored by the PCM–SGTD); Ley de Gobierno Digital (Decreto Legislativo 1412); Marco de Confianza Digital (DU 007-2020) and its regulation DS 126-2025-PCM (published 4 Nov 2025, in force 3 Feb 2026); DS 098-2025-PCM on digital identity, e-signature and digital procedures; Law 31814 promoting AI and its regulation DS 115-2025-PCM (Sep 2025), which requires a National AI Strategy to 2030 to be approved within one year

Building blocks

AreaWhat existsStatus
Digital identityRENIEC approved guidelines for the digital national ID (DNId) in Aug 2026, meeting the 31 Jul deadline set by DS 098-2025-PCM, and launched free issuance in Nov 2026 (characteristics of the DNId and the Perú Pass wallet approved by Resolución Jefatural D000076-2026-RENIEC/JNAC on 18 Nov 2026). The DNId has the same legal value, legal effect and evidential weight as the physical DNI, which remains valid. It is held in the Perú Pass wallet (Android and iOS), built on the open-source Inji platform, and is voluntary. Adults with a valid DNI enrol by reading the card’s MRZ and passing fingerprint and face checks in the ID Perú app; it is valid for one year and parents can obtain it for minors. Verification uses temporary, cryptographically signed dynamic QR codes (photos and screenshots are not valid); holders share only the data each procedure needs and a lost phone’s credential can be revoked on RENIEC’s website. Digital paymentsBCRP: about 655 digital payments per adult in 2025, up 45.8% on 2024. Alias-based instant payments (by phone number) rose 63% and made up 73.6% of low-value payments. CCE instant transfers grew 119.3% in number and 40.7% in value in 2025, averaging about 45 million transactions and S/ 20,964 million a month. BCRP-mandated interoperability: phase 1 (Yape–Plin, since Mar 2023) passed 180 million transactions in Dec 2025; phases 1–2 (bank apps and QR codes, since Sep 2023) together exceeded 263 million in Dec 2025; phase 3 (e-money issuers) handled about 8 million a month. Merchant QR payments reached 47 million (S/ 1,838 million) in Dec 2025, up 74%. The BCRP’s Retail Payments Platform is due to go live in Dec 2026Operational
Data exchangeThe Plataforma de Interoperabilidad del Estado (PIDE), run by the PCM–SGTD, recorded more than 500 million transactions by Q4 2025; 267 entities were interconnected under the “Cero Papel” paperless strategyOperational
Civil registration (CRVS)RENIEC keeps the civil register. In 2025 it recorded 676,715 civil registrations (430,385 births, 181,270 deaths, 65,060 marriages) and issued 2,401,814 certified copies of civil records, about 3.08 million procedures in all. [To verify: share of births registered digitally in hospitals and online channels for certified copies]Operational
Business registrySUNARP’s Sistema de Intermediación Digital (SID-SUNARP) became mandatory in Apr 2020 for forming SAs, SRLs, SCRLs and EIRLs, starting in Lima and Callao, with registry review in no more than two business days. From May 2023 to 15 May 2026, 37,122 companies were formed through Centros de Desarrollo Empresarial with no registry fee; DS 008-2026-PRODUCE extended the fee exemption for three yearsOperational
Government services portalgob.pe is Peru’s single portal for digital government services; the PCM reported in Jan 2026 that it had been consolidated as the national channel for digital public services. [To verify: number of entities and online procedures on gob.pe]Operational
Data protectionLey 29733 (Personal Data Protection Law) is enforced by the ANPD. Its new regulation, DS 016-2024-JUS, took effect on 31 Mar 2025 and covers public and private processing; it requires security incidents affecting many people or involving sensitive data to be reported to the ANPD within 48 hours and sets a free, simple opt-out from marketing. In Dec 2025–Jan 2026 the ANPD set criteria for when a data protection officer must be appointed and a fine-calculation methodologyOperational
CybersecurityThe Marco de Confianza Digital (DU 007-2020) regulation, DS 126-2025-PCM, was published on 4 Nov 2025 and took effect on 3 Feb 2026: digital service providers must report security incidents to the Centro Nacional de Seguridad Digital (CNSD) within 48 hours, and also to the ANPD if personal data is involved. In Q4 2025 the CNSD issued more than 1,000 alerts and gave 1,279 technical assistances. [To verify: current role of PeCERT within the CNSD]Operational
AI governanceLaw 31814 promotes AI for economic and social development; its regulation, DS 115-2025-PCM, published on 9 Sep 2025 with the PCM–SGTD as lead authority, bans “improper uses” (such as manipulation, mass surveillance without legal basis and real-time biometric identification in public spaces, with exceptions), lists high-risk uses (critical infrastructure, hiring and dismissal, education of minors, social programmes, credit scoring, health access) with mandatory human oversight, and phases in obligations over 1 to 4 years. The SGTD must draft a National AI Strategy to 2030. Access Now and Hiperderecho submitted civil-society comments on the draft in May 2024Law or policy
