United States: digital government profile

Reviewed 2026-11-20 · UN E-Government Development Index: 19 of 193 · 0.920 · 2024

Lead agency
No single digital ministry. General Services Administration (Administrator Edward Forst): Technology Transformation Services (TTS), whose acting director since Aug 2026 is Treasury CIO Sam Corcos, who is also acting head of Login.gov (TTS has shed about 70% of its staff since Jan 2025); Office of Management and Budget, Office of the Federal CIO (Greg Barbaccia left at end Aug 2026; EPA CIO Carter Farmer reported as the White House’s pick in Sep 2026 [To verify: formal appointment]); National Design Studio in the Executive Office of the President (EO 14338, 21 Aug 2025; Chief Design Officer Joe Gebbia), which runs America.gov with GSA and OMB; U.S. DOGE Service (the former U.S. Digital Service, renamed 20 Jan 2025; about 70 permanent staff in Jan 2026), whose temporary ‘DOGE’ organisation reached its 4 Jul 2026 end date with about two dozen DOGE recruits still in government; Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA, DHS), led by acting director Nick Andersen since Feb 2026, with Palantir CISO Michael LoSapio nominated as director on 9 Nov 2026; OPM’s U.S. Tech Force recruitment programme
Strategy
No single digital-government strategy; policy is set by executive orders and OMB memoranda: America’s AI Action Plan (Jul 2025); OMB M-25-21 (AI use) and M-25-22 (AI acquisition), 3 Apr 2025, replacing M-24-10; OMB M-26-04 on ‘unbiased AI principles’ in procurement (11 Dec 2025); EO on a national policy framework for AI, aimed at pre-empting state AI laws (11 Dec 2025), followed by a National AI Legislative Framework sent to Congress (20 Mar 2026); EO 14338 ‘Improving Our Nation Through Better Design’ (America by Design, 21 Aug 2025); EO on advanced AI innovation and security (2 Jun 2026); EO ‘Streamlining Access to Government Services Through America.gov’ (29 Sep 2026); EO ‘Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence’ (29 Sep 2026)

Building blocks

AreaWhat existsStatus
Digital identityLogin.gov (GSA): 180+ million user accounts, 500+ million sign-ins a year, 54 federal and state agencies and 700+ sites (roadmap, Aug 2026); a draft OMB memo (Aug 2026) would make agencies offer Login.gov for most public-facing authenticated services, and the America.gov order (29 Sep 2026) directs its integration there; REAL ID enforced since 7 May 2025, with TSA’s $45 ConfirmID check for travellers without one from 1 Feb 2026 (valid 10 days); state mobile driver’s licences: 21 states plus Puerto Rico offer one and 250+ TSA checkpoints accept eligible digital IDs (Oct 2026) [To verify: TSA’s own participating-states page lists only 11 states]Operational
Digital paymentsFedNow (Federal Reserve, live since 20 Jul 2023): more than 1,800 participating banks and credit unions (Jul 2026); limit per credit transfer raised to $10 million in Nov 2025 (from $1 million); Q3 2026: 6,376,467 settled payments worth $370.9 billion (+27.6% in volume on Q2), about 69,300 a day; 2025: 8.41 million payments worth $853.4 billion; The Clearing House’s RTP network: 128 million payments worth $480 billion in Q1 2026, more than 1,200 institutions, $10 million limit since Feb 2025Operational
Data exchangeNo national data-exchange layer: sharing runs through bilateral and programme-specific links; NAPHSIS’s EVVE hub lets SSA, the State Department, DHS, CMS and state DMVs verify birth and death records in seconds; the America.gov order (29 Sep 2026) tells OMB to guide agencies on bringing covered services (over 100,000 users a year) into one front door with Login.gov; 39 vital-records jurisdictions have completed FHIR mortality pre-certification with NCHS (Jun 2026) [To verify: implementation of EO 14243 on cross-agency data access (Mar 2025)]Partial
Civil registrationBirths, deaths and fetal deaths are registered by 57 jurisdictions (50 states, New York City, DC and 5 territories) and compiled by CDC’s National Center for Health Statistics in the National Vital Statistics System (about 6.8 million events a year); 39 jurisdictions FHIR pre-certified for mortality data (Jun 2026); EVVE and EVVE Fact of Death (NAPHSIS) give authorised agencies and approved firms yes/no checks across participating jurisdictionsOperational
Business registryCompany formation is state-based (secretaries of state), with no national business register; FinCEN’s final rule of 14 Aug 2026 (FR doc. 2026-16576) made permanent the Mar 2025 interim rule: U.S.-formed companies and U.S. persons no longer report beneficial ownership under the Corporate Transparency Act, only foreign reporting companies do, and FinCEN plans a one-time deletion of U.S. persons’ data already heldPartial
Government services portalAmerica.gov, launched 29 Sep 2026 by the National Design Studio and GSA: an AI assistant built on commercial AI models answering questions from about 29,000 federal websites; passport renewal and Medicare enrolment promised ‘later this year’ and full transactions by 2027; covers federal services with more than 100,000 users a year (IRS filing excluded) [To verify: the future of USA.gov]Partial
Data protectionNo comprehensive federal privacy law; sectoral federal laws (Privacy Act 1974 for federal agencies, HIPAA, GLBA, COPPA) and state comprehensive consumer-privacy laws in about 20 states, led by California’s CCPA/CPRA [To verify: current count of state laws and compliance date of the amended FTC COPPA Rule]To verify
CybersecurityCISA (DHS) leads civilian federal and critical-infrastructure cybersecurity under FISMA 2014; no Senate-confirmed director since Jan 2025 (Sean Plankey withdrew Apr 2026; Michael LoSapio nominated 9 Nov 2026); workforce cut by about a third, with some 300–600 new hires planned (Jun 2026); CIRCIA final rule (72-hour incident and 24-hour ransom-payment reports) sent to OMB for review 1 Oct 2026, not yet published; EO of 2 Jun 2026 sets AI-enabled cyber defence priorities and a vulnerability clearinghousePartial
AI governanceNo federal AI statute; OMB M-25-21/M-25-22 (3 Apr 2025) require agency Chief AI Officers, AI use-case inventories and minimum practices for ‘high-impact’ AI, and M-26-04 adds ‘unbiased AI’ contract terms (agency policies due 11 Mar 2026); EO of 11 Dec 2025 created a DOJ AI Litigation Task Force against state AI laws, and DOJ joined a federal challenge to Colorado’s 2024 AI Act; Colorado replaced that act with SB 26-189 on automated decision-making technology (signed 14 May 2026, in force 1 Jan 2027); California SB 53, the Transparency in Frontier AI Act (signed 29 Sep 2025, in force 1 Jan 2026), requires frontier developers to publish safety frameworks and report critical incidents to Cal OES within 15 days (fines up to $1 million); voluntary frontier-safety accord signed at the White House 29 Sep 2026Law or policy
