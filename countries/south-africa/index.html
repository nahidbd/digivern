South Africa: digital government profile

Reviewed 2026-11-23 · UN E-Government Development Index: 40 of 193 · 0.862 · 2024

Lead agency
Department of Communications and Digital Technologies (DCDT), co-chairing the Inter-Departmental Working Group on digital transformation with National Treasury; Operation Vulindlela (Presidency and National Treasury) leads the Roadmap, with its digital services unit building MyMzansi
Strategy
Roadmap for the Digital Transformation of Government (launched May 2025 under Operation Vulindlela Phase II, itself launched 7 May 2025): "One Person, One Government, One Touch", four pillars (digital identity, data exchange, digital payments, digital services); Phase 1 (2025–2027) social protection, Phase 2 (2028–2030) health, education and business services

Building blocks

AreaWhat existsStatus
Digital identitySmart ID card, Department of Home Affairs (DHA): digital partnership with banks since 9 Mar 2026; 1,002,773 replacement Smart IDs obtained at 669 bank branches by 26 Oct 2026, and about 1.16 million applications at 833 branches by 18 Nov 2026 (750-branch target passed more than four months before its 31 Mar 2027 deadline); from 18 Nov 2026 adults can also apply for passports at 480 bank branches (Capitec 412, Absa 68), taking passport points from 228 Home Affairs offices to 708; green ID book production ends 31 Mar 2027 and the book stops being valid ID on 31 Mar 2028 (about 14.7 million people still relied on it in Oct 2026)Operational
Digital ID (mobile credentials)Draft Digital Identity regulations under the Identification Act 1997 published for comment on 5 May 2026 (comments to 6 Jun 2026): optional digital ID, birth and marriage certificates on a smartphone, remote biometric verification, with the population register as the single authoritative identity record; working prototype unveiled 1 Oct 2026 (QR code or NFC check with biometric confirmation); DHA targets hosting infrastructure by 31 Mar 2027 and credentials in a mobile wallet in 2027/28Partial
Digital paymentsPayShap real-time payments (launched 2023, South African Reserve Bank): more than 1 billion transactions and over R1 trillion transferred (SARB Governor, 20 Oct 2026); about 6% of card transaction volumes and under 40% of EFT credit volumes last year; the Roadmap plans end-to-end government-to-person payments for Child Support and SRD grants by Q4 2026Operational
Data exchangeMzansiXchange, National Treasury: one-year pilot launched 9 Oct 2025, built on X-Road; a federated exchange (not a central database) for real-time verification and de-identified research data; the Roadmap targets real-time grant-eligibility checks and a Master Social Security Registry by Q4 2026 and 20 more databases by Q4 2027Partial
Civil registration (CRVS)Births registered by the Department of Home Affairs: 863,858 births registered in 2024, 83.2% within 30 days of birth; births eligible for the National Population Register are captured there and get an electronic birth certificate (Stats SA, 30 Oct 2025)Operational
Business registryCompanies and Intellectual Property Commission (CIPC): 515,892 company registrations in 2025/26, up from 488,355 in 2024/25, with an average turnaround of 1 day (CIPC Annual Report 2025/26)Operational
Government services portalMyMzansi single access platform (Operation Vulindlela digital services unit): the Roadmap set launch for Q2 2026, 20 priority services by Q4 2027 and 50 by Q4 2030; as of 21 Oct 2026 no public launch date, with R150 million allocated in the adjustments budget; the prototype completed a driving licence renewal linked to Natis; gov.za remains the main portal, and DHA launched myhomeaffairsonline.dha.gov.za (3 Aug 2026) to find bank branches for Smart ID servicesPartial
Data protectionProtection of Personal Information Act 2013 (POPIA), enforced by the Information Regulator: R5 million fines each on the Department of Justice (2021 cyberattack exposing personal data) and the Department of Basic Education (matric results published in newspapers), briefed 31 Aug 2026; both disputed in courtLaw or policy
CybersecurityCybercrimes Act 19 of 2020: most provisions in force since 1 Dec 2021 (Government Gazette No. 45562), creating offences such as unlawful access, interception, cyber fraud, extortion and malicious communications; section 54 (obligations of electronic communications service providers and financial institutions), protection orders, mutual assistance and the designated point of contact were excluded from that commencement [To verify: whether section 54 has since commenced]Law or policy
AI governanceDraft National AI Policy (DCDT) withdrawn: the minister announced in April 2026 that the draft published for comment cited fictitious sources, and Cabinet approved its withdrawal on 5 Jun 2026 so it can be reworked; no new date announcedPartial
