Mexico: digital government profile

Reviewed 2026-11-24 · UN E-Government Development Index: 65 of 193 · 0.785 · 2024

Lead agency
Agencia de Transformación Digital y Telecomunicaciones (ATDT), headed by José Antonio Peña Merino: created by a reform of the Ley Orgánica de la Administración Pública Federal (DOF 28 Nov 2024); it is the Autoridad Nacional de Simplificación y Digitalización under the Ley Nacional para Eliminar Trámites Burocráticos (2025), runs Llave MX, the Expediente Digital Ciudadano and the single citizen portal, and leads telecom policy, with the Comisión Reguladora de Telecomunicaciones (CRT) as its regulator; 2026 budget proposal MXN 3,852 million (+23.6% real), 88.7% of it for telecoms
Strategy
Programa Sectorial de la ATDT 2025–2030 (DOF 19 Sep 2025, under the Plan Nacional de Desarrollo 2025–2030), with six priority objectives: internet access (rural areas first), digital ID and simplified digital procedures, security of federal digital systems, the space programme, public technology and data sovereignty, and postal services. Related plans: the Plan Nacional de Conectividad 2026–2030 (DOF, Apr 2026) to raise internet coverage from 94% to 98% of the population by 2030, and the Plan Nacional de Ciberseguridad 2025–2030 (presented Jan 2026)

AreaWhat existsStatus
Digital identityLlave MX, the single government login run by the ATDT: 28,048,244 accounts by July 2026 (2.96 million created in one week during Beca Rita Cetina sign-up; about 4.5 million users abroad), 242 systems integrated (204 federal, 30 state, 8 municipal), and 72.7% of access by mobile. CURP biométrica (photo, fingerprints, iris and signature linked to the 18-character CURP): enrolment piloted by Segob (RENAPO) from April 2026 with its own staff first, then gradual roll-out at RENAPO and Registro Civil modules; registration is voluntary and free, with no deadline, and the old CURP stays valid (May 2026) [To verify: number of biometric CURPs issued to date]Partial
Legal status of digital IDReform of the Ley General de Población (DOF 16 Jul 2025) makes the CURP with biometric data the national identity document (it does not replace the INE voter card for elections) and gave Segob 90 days to build a Plataforma Única de Identidad. The Ley Nacional para Eliminar Trámites Burocráticos (DOF 16 Jul 2025) defines Llave MX as the means of digital authentication and identification (art. 64), requires every account to be linked to a CURP (art. 65), and requires public bodies and private firms that authenticate people for procedures to accept it (arts. 16 IX, 74, 75). Segob says no institution may make services conditional on the biometric CURP during the transitionLaw or policy
Digital paymentsSPEI, Banco de México's real-time payment system: 7,308.1 million transfers worth MXN 591.8 trillion in 2025 (about 16.8 times GDP); end-user transfers rose from 5,336.7 million (2024) to 7,302.4 million (about +37%), 94.6% of them low-value; 94 participants, and non-bank financial institutions handle 19.8% of operations. DiMo (pay by mobile number): 16.4 million registered users and 3.1 million transfers (MXN 3.7 billion) in 2025. CoDi (QR): 4.4 million operations, 279,400 active accounts in Q4 2025 (Banxico, Apr 2026)Operational
Data exchangeExpediente Digital Ciudadano (Ley Nacional para Eliminar Trámites Burocráticos, art. 76): an ATDT-run tool that lets the databases and systems of public bodies interoperate for consultation and data portability, with the holder's consent; public bodies may not ask for information already in the Expediente or documents they already hold (art. 78). Expediente MX accounted for 7.46% of Llave MX logins by June 2026. The Programa Sectorial 2025–2030 lists lack of interoperability as a problem but sets no numeric targetPartial
Civil registration (CRVS)Births registered with the state civil registries: 1,678,532 in 2025, 78.5% of them before the child's first birthday (INEGI, 25 Sep 2026). The Plataforma Nacional del Registro Civil is the most used Llave MX service, with 22.91% of logins (ATDT, Jul 2026)Operational
Business registrySociedad por Acciones Simplificada (SAS): formed online through gob.mx/tuempresa (Secretaría de Economía) without a notary, taking legal effect on entry in the Registro Público de Comercio; 2026 annual revenue cap MXN 7,678,849.94 (DOF 26 Dec 2025); since 2026 SAT tax registration (RFC) requires an in-person appointment within a month of incorporation [To verify: annual number of SAS incorporations]Operational
Government services portalgob.mx with single sign-on through Llave MX; the Ley Nacional para Eliminar Trámites Burocráticos (2025) makes the ATDT administer a Portal Ciudadano Único de Trámites y Servicios and a Repositorio Nacional de Tecnología Pública, and requires procedure websites to use the Llave MX login (arts. 9, 74, 87). Most used services by July 2026: Registro Civil (22.91%), Llave MX itself (19.72%), Expediente MX (7.46%) and Foreign Ministry appointments (6.89%)Operational
Data protectionNew Ley Federal de Protección de Datos Personales en Posesión de los Particulares (DOF 20 Mar 2025, in force 21 Mar 2025; last amended 14 Nov 2025): the autonomous INAI's functions and staff passed to the Secretaría Anticorrupción y Buen Gobierno (SABG), now the supervisory authority; fines up to 320,000 UMA, doubled for sensitive data. SABG enforcement report (Oct 2026): 51 private parties fined MXN 118.5 million; 122 investigations and 60 inspections of public bodies (Sep 2025–Jun 2026), including IMSS, SAT and SHF data incidents; it says it has absorbed 80% of INAI's functionsLaw or policy
CybersecurityPlan Nacional de Ciberseguridad 2025–2030 (ATDT Directorate General of Cybersecurity, presented Jan 2026): six phases from Fundamento (2025) to Transformación (2030) and six axes; plans a national CSIRT and a Centro Nacional de Operaciones de Ciberseguridad (CNSOC) to coordinate more than 60 CERT/CSIRT teams. The Ley General de Ciberseguridad the roadmap set for the second half of 2026 was still pending in Congress at 31 Oct 2026. Mexico had 155 published ransomware victims (Nov 2019–Sep 2025), second in the region after BrazilPartial
AI governanceNo general AI law (Aug 2026). Pending bills include a constitutional reform to article 73 so Congress can legislate on AI (Chamber of Deputies and Senate, Apr 2026), a federal law on ethical, sovereign and inclusive AI with a National AI Council and an algorithmic audit platform (Chamber, 24 Jul 2026), and sector bills on telecoms (Feb 2026) and labour (Apr 2026); the ATDT's 2026 goals include AI digital assistants for government proceduresPartial
Telecom and connectivityLey en Materia de Telecomunicaciones y Radiodifusión (DOF 16 Jul 2025, in force 17 Jul 2025) repealed the 2014 law and replaced the autonomous IFT with the Comisión Reguladora de Telecomunicaciones (CRT), part of the ATDT. CFE Telecomunicaciones e Internet para Todos runs 101,288 free internet access points (71,461 on 4G, 16,584 by satellite), over 18,000 km of backbone fibre and 5,253 towers. 83.1% of people aged 6+ use the internet (100.2 million), with 86.9% urban against 68.5% rural use and 20.4 million offline (ENDUTIH 2024). The Plan Nacional de Conectividad 2026–2030 targets 98% coverage and about 91,000 more localities by 2030Operational
