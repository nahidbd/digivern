Afghanistan: digital government profile

Reviewed 2026-11-18 · UN E-Government Development Index: 188 of 193 · 0.208 · 2024

Lead agency
Taliban-run Ministry of Communications and Information Technology (MCIT, whose Digital Governance Directorate oversees digital governance nationwide), National Statistics and Information Authority (NSIA: e-Tazkira and Asan Khedmat 'Easy Service' centres) and Da Afghanistan Bank (DAB) for payments, under the Taliban-run de facto administration
Strategy
No published national digital strategy found under the de facto authorities; a general strategy for expanding electronic payments, approved by the Prime Minister's Office, and a decree by the Taliban leader on collecting revenue through digital systems are being implemented by DAB with an action plan (Jan 2026); the World Bank Digital CASA project (IDA US$51m, including US$10m for e-government, implemented by MCIT, MoF and ATRA) had produced no e-government results by Oct 2021

Building blocks

AreaWhat existsStatus
Digital identitye-Tazkira electronic national ID card run by NSIA, issued since May 2018: nearly 18.3 million cards issued, including 827,060 in Jan–Jun 2026 (600,546 to men, 226,514 to women); about 2 million e-IDs distributed in the 1447 lunar year (1.5 million to men, 500,000 to women) per the de facto administration's annual report (Aug 2026); 87 distribution centres plus one in Abu Dhabi; fee AFN 500 (AFN 700 at Asan Khedmat centres). System restored after several days of technical disruption in Jan 2026. Returnees and women face barriers (cost, travel to home province, male guardian approval)Operational
Digital paymentsAfghanistan Payments System (APS), DAB's national e-payment switch operating since Jan 2021, with the domestic Afpay card scheme and payments for customs, income tax and Breshna electricity bills; DAB rebuilt the systems in-house after 2021 and agreed with the Ministry of Finance in Mar 2025 to link APS and ACSS to ASYCUDA, SIGTAS and AFMIS for electronic revenue collection and salaries; in Jan 2026 DAB began implementing a Prime Minister's Office-approved strategy for expanding electronic payments and a decree on digital revenue collection, with an action plan for e-money and payment institutionsOperational
Mobile money / e-money8 electronic money institutions licensed by DAB, from M-Paisa (2013) to HesabPay (2023), Star Pay (Jan 2025) and Las Pay (Oct 2025), plus 1 payment institution (Refah); HesabPay mobile vouchers delivered aid to 2,409 very poor female-headed households in Kabul, Herat and Mazar-i-Sharif in Nov 2022–Feb 2023 at 6.7 cents per dollar, an approach WFP has since adopted for 14,000 householdsOperational
Government servicesAsan Khedmat ('Easy Service') one-stop centres (set up by MCIT in 2015, run by NSIA since 2020) issue e-Tazkira, birth and death certificates and consular services: 4 in Kabul plus Kandahar and Khost (Jan 2025), new centres in Balkh, Herat, Helmand and Nangarhar (2024–25) and Shahr-e Naw, Kabul, with Farah and Kunduz nearing completion (Aug 2026), and one in the UAE; some services moved online in the 1447 lunar year (passport registration, work permits, paper-ID corrections used by about 214,000 people); no national online services portal found (World Bank Digital CASA target of a national portal and 4 e-services had not been met by Oct 2021)Partial
Civil registration (CRVS)Birth and death certificates issued by NSIA, including through Asan Khedmat centres: more than 126,000 birth certificates and about 25,000 death certificates in the 1447 lunar year (de facto administration annual report, Aug 2026); [To verify: national coverage and digitisation of civil registration — the latest coverage figure found, 42% of under-fives with a birth certificate, dates from UNICEF 2017]Partial
Business registryCentral Business Registry and Intellectual Property General Directorate of the Ministry of Industry and Commerce, which in 2020 began building a one-stop-shop licensing database across 20 ministries; licences are still issued by several bodies (Ministries of Industry and Commerce, Justice and Agriculture, trade unions, municipalities); no online public business registry found under the de facto authoritiesPartial
Connectivity and internet shutdownsDe facto authorities banned fibre-optic internet in Balkh from 16 Sep 2025 and in at least 14 provinces by 18 Sep, then imposed a nationwide internet and phone shutdown from 29 Sep 2025, restored on 1 Oct 2025 after about 48 hours, during which airports, banks and post offices could not operate; in Aug 2026 the Taliban-run communications ministry reportedly ordered Afghan Telecom and private firms to halt fibre-optic expansion and repairs (single leaked document), while the de facto administration reported 851 km of fibre laid and about 350 new telecom antennas in the 1447 lunar yearPartial
Data protection[To verify: no data protection law or authority found]To verify
CybersecurityCyber Crime Law (28 articles, part of the revised penal code) signed in Jul 2017 under the previous government; [To verify: whether the de facto authorities apply it, and any cybersecurity strategy or national CERT]To verify
Development partnersWorld Bank Digital CASA project (MCIT, IDA US$51m) rated unsatisfactory in Oct 2021 with 6.7% disbursed; IOM lists more than 86% of 6.1 million returnees since Sep 2023 as undocumented, and a UN initiative launched in Jun 2026 aims to help Afghans obtain 1.5 million identity documents over three years; NRC supports returnees to obtain the Tazkira (Mar 2026 briefing); digital aid via HesabPay coordinated by Uplift Afghanistan and evaluated by J-PAL researchersPartial
