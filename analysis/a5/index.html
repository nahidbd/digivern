Twenty-five crore accounts, one in three adults left out: what Bangladesh’s national financial inclusion survey says

21 Nov 2026 · 17 min read · 55 sources

Bangladesh’s National Financial Inclusion Survey 2025, presented in Dhaka on 18 November, puts formal financial inclusion at 64.5% of adults — short of the 100% the government aimed for by June 2026. Mobile money accounts have grown sevenfold in a decade, yet only 21% of users use an app and more than half of women have no phone of their own. How the country got here, what the numbers show, what India, Pakistan and Kenya did, and what should come next.

Key numbers

  • 64.5% adults formally financially included, National Financial Inclusion Survey 20251
  • 25 crore MFS accounts, 10 crore of them held by women1
  • 40% share of about 230 million MFS accounts that are active (World Bank, 2025)48
  • 43% adults with an account on the World Bank’s Global Findex measure (2024 data)6
  • Tk 2.01 lakh crore MFS transactions in April 2026 alone, up from Tk 98,307 crore in July 202353
  • 2.72 crore agent banking accounts in June 2026, about half held by women16

The short version

Bangladesh now has a national baseline for financial inclusion, and it shows both how far the country has come and how far it still has to go. Findings first reported on 10–11 November from Bangladesh Bank’s Financial Inclusion Report 2025 put formal financial inclusion at 64.5% of adults 1; the National Financial Inclusion Survey 2025 behind them was presented at the InterContinental Dhaka on 18 November, with M Abdur Razzaque, chairman of Research and Policy Integration for Development (RAPID), giving the keynote 23. That leaves 35.5% of adults outside the formal financial system 4 — against a government goal of financial inclusion for all adults by June 2026 7.

The survey’s sharpest findings are about use, not access. Only 8% of bank account holders use debit cards and just 21% of MFS account holders use mobile apps; about 34% of MFS users transact on basic feature phones, and bill payments and other services make up less than 4% of MFS use, because most people mainly send and receive money 2. And 55.7% of women in Bangladesh do not own a personal mobile phone, compared with 82.5% of men who do 2. “The largest deficit in financial inclusion is visible among women,” Razzaque said 2.

This matters because Bangladesh is building its next generation of payment infrastructure — an Inclusive Instant Payment System due by July 2027 89 — on the assumption that people can and will pay digitally. The survey says the binding constraints are now phones, skills, trust and cost, not the number of accounts.

Background and history: from Tk 10 accounts to 25 crore wallets

Bangladesh’s modern push began with the banks. In September 2010 Bangladesh Bank instructed public banks to help the ultra-poor open Tk 10 accounts; by 30 June 2017 banks had opened 1.71 crore such accounts holding Tk 1,311 crore 25. In November 2010 the central bank asked commercial banks to introduce school banking to build a savings habit among students 24.

Mobile money followed. Bangladesh Bank’s September 2011 guidelines on ‘Mobile Financial Services for Banks’ chose a bank-led model 17. bKash, established in 2011, had 29 million users by December 2017 26. Agent banking came next: the central bank issued its agent banking guidelines in PSD Circular No. 05 on 9 December 2013 18, and Bank Asia opened the first outlet on 17 January 2014 19. The Bangladesh Post Office’s Nagad was launched on 26 March 2019 20.

Rules and strategy then caught up with the market. New MFS Regulations in February 2022 replaced those of 2018 21. The cabinet cleared a draft national financial inclusion strategy in November 2020 with the aim of bringing ‘100 per cent people under the financial accounting system’ 28; the final strategy runs from 1 July 2021 to 30 June 2026 with 12 goals and 69 targets 27. Parliament passed the Payment and Settlement System Act on 2 July 2024, with penalties of up to five years in prison or a Tk 5 million fine for violations 22, and in November 2025 Bangladesh Bank published draft rules for e-money issuers that would open digital payments to non-bank firms with at least Tk 50 crore of paid-up capital 23.

What the data show: rapid growth in accounts, slower growth in use

Accounts have multiplied. Registered MFS accounts rose from 3.39 crore in February 2016 15 to 7.05 crore in May 2019 14, 19.10 crore in December 2022 13, 23.87 crore at the end of 2024 54 and 25 crore in the latest count 1 — roughly a sevenfold increase in a decade (GovTec calculation). Women’s share has grown: in December 2022, 8.01 crore users were women against 11.04 crore men 13; now 10 crore of 25 crore accounts are held by women 1.

Money flows have grown faster still. Monthly MFS transactions rose from Tk 16,568.89 crore in February 2016 15 to Tk 42,236.23 crore in May 2019 14, Tk 96,132 crore in December 2022 13, Tk 1,24,548 crore in December 2023 55 and Tk 2,01,337 crore in April 2026 53 — about 12 times the 2016 level (GovTec calculation). In April–June 2026 MFS handled Tk 6.60 lakh crore, about two-thirds of it through bKash 52.

But activity has not kept pace. In February 2016, 59% of MFS accounts were inactive 15. In May 2019, 3.21 crore of 7.05 crore accounts were active — about 46% (GovTec calculation) 14. Bangladesh Bank counted 88.89 million active accounts against 238.68 million registered at the end of 2024, about 37% (GovTec calculation) 54, and the World Bank’s Gayle Martin wrote in 2025 that ‘only 40 percent of 230 million MFS accounts are active’ 48. One researcher warns that rising totals do not by themselves show cash being displaced and that Bangladesh Bank should publish clearer counts and definitions 53.

The survey adds texture by group. Account ownership rises from 55.1% among adults with no formal education to nearly 90% among those with tertiary education 1. Women use accounts more than men for savings (62.7% against 58.4%) and for receiving remittances (26.1% against 11.5%), while men more often receive salaries through them (29.6% against 18.9%) 2. Around 70% of women and 59% of men have no knowledge of investment diversification 2. In 2025 accounts grew 10% at banks, 19% at finance companies, 8% at MFS providers and 7% at microfinance institutions 1.

International measures tell a harsher story. On the World Bank’s cross-country Global Findex, 43% of Bangladeshi adults had an account in 2024, against a global average of 73% cited by the World Bank’s Jean Pesme 6; 21% held a mobile money account and 8% a debit card 30, and 34% made digital payments 29. Findex also shows 74% of Bangladeshi women owned a mobile phone against 91% of men, and only 27% of women made or received a digital payment against 45% of men 31. The gap between 64.5% and 43% is largely one of method and definition: the two surveys count different things, and neither is wrong.

The phone gap is now the inclusion gap

Bangladesh’s inclusion model runs through the mobile phone, so phone ownership sets the ceiling. GSMA’s Mobile Gender Gap Report 2025 found that 42% of men but only 26% of women in Bangladesh use mobile internet — a 39% gender gap 37. The national survey’s finding that 55.7% of women lack their own phone 2 explains why only a minority of users reach app-based services such as bill payments, savings or credit.

Policy is pulling in two directions here. The government plans low-cost hybrid smartphones that could bring the effective price down to Tk 2,000–3,000, against about Tk 9,000 for the cheapest smartphones now; an ICT adviser to the government said ‘fifty per cent of people in our country still do not use data phones’ 46. At the same time the proposed 2026–27 budget would raise customs duty on imported phones from 10% to 25%, lifting the total tax burden from 43.43% to 64.25% 45.

Distance and cost also bite. bKash CEO Kamal Quadir said ‘transportation costs make accessing financial services difficult’ for rural people who must travel to distant bank branches 3. Agent banking helps: by June 2026 it had 2.72 crore accounts, about half held by women, and Tk 51,907 crore in deposits through 20,597 outlets (GovTec conversion of Tk 519,072.92 million) 16, with 86% of outlets and 85% of accounts in rural areas 1.

What other countries did

India went for accounts first at enormous scale. Its Pradhan Mantri Jan Dhan Yojana (PMJDY) had 59.09 crore accounts by 19 August 2026, of which 32.92 crore belonged to women 41, but women still trail men in phone ownership (54% against 77%) and digital payments (36% against 54%) 31.

Pakistan set explicit targets for women. Its National Financial Inclusion Strategy 2024–28 aims to raise inclusion to 75% and cut the gender gap to 25% by 2028, from 64% inclusion in 2023 38. The State Bank’s Banking on Equality Policy 2026 records the gender gap falling from 39% in 2021 to 29% in December 2025 and targets 35,000 women branchless-banking agents 39. Pakistan’s phone gap is wider than Bangladesh’s: 24% of women own a phone against 89% of men 32.

Kenya shows what mobile money can do when phones are near-universal: formal inclusion reached 84.8% in 2024, with exclusion down to 9.9%, and the central bank says ‘the key driver of financial inclusion remains mobile money’ 40. Across South Asia, GSMA counts 473 million registered and 110 million 30-day active mobile money accounts in 2025, handling $304 billion 35; globally there were 2.3 billion registered and 593 million active accounts, with more than $2 trillion transacted 34. GSMA also finds the mobile money gender gap in South Asia narrowing from 75% in 2017 to 63% in 2021 and 59% now 36.

Where Bangladesh stands now

Bangladesh has the infrastructure for mass inclusion — 25 crore MFS accounts, over 20 lakh active agents, 11,418 bank branches and 20,501 agent banking outlets 1 — and a regulator now focused on interoperability. Since November 2025 transfers between banks, MFS providers and payment service providers can run through the National Payment Switch Bangladesh, with fee caps of 0.85% from MFS and 0.15% from banks 50; the system started without bKash and Nagad, and Governor Ahsan H Mansur conceded later that month that the interoperable system ‘has not been successful’ because many institutions were not using it 51. Bangladesh Bank now targets July 2027 for the Inclusive Instant Payment System, built with the Gates Foundation’s Mojaloop 89.

The national strategy’s period ended in June 2026 without reaching its headline goal. By December 2024, 48 of its 69 targets were fully implemented, 19 partially and two not at all 54; in December 2024 only 37.7% of women owned a bank deposit account 7. GovTec could not find a published successor strategy. Bangladesh Bank has, however, revamped a Tk 1,500 crore refinance scheme for inclusion: banks borrow at 1%, lend up to Tk 10 lakh at no more than 7%, and must give at least 25% of loans to women 10.

Outlook: what comes next

The announced milestones are the IIPS launch in July 2027 9, final rules for non-bank e-money issuers 23, the low-cost smartphone programme 46 and the fate of the proposed phone duty increase 45. A successor to the 2021–2026 strategy is the obvious missing piece.

GovTec’s view — best case: a new strategy sets targets for active use and for women, IIPS launches on time with bKash and Nagad connected, and cheap smartphones reach poor households, pushing inclusion well above 70% and app use far above 21%. Base case: account numbers keep rising, IIPS launches but large wallets join slowly, and active use and the gender gap improve only gradually. Worst case: higher phone taxes and cash-out costs keep women and rural users on basic phones and agents, interoperability stalls again as it did in 2025 51, and the 35.5% excluded become a hard core that digital channels never reach.

GovTec’s assessment

GovTec’s view: Bangladesh has largely solved the account problem and now faces a use problem. Three choices will decide the next five years. First, measure what matters: future targets should track active use, digital payments and women’s phone ownership, not registered accounts — and Bangladesh Bank should publish consistent definitions, as researchers have asked 53. Second, treat phones as financial infrastructure: taxing imported handsets more heavily 45 while subsidising cheap smartphones 46 sends mixed signals. Third, bring down the cost of cash-out and cross-platform transfers, which still deter workers paid digitally 4742. The 2025 survey gives Bangladesh, for the first time, a baseline to hold itself to; the test is whether the next strategy uses it.

Money and value

Who pays for cash: Governor Ahsan H Mansur put the cost of managing cash at about Tk 200 billion a year 12. Every taka that stays digital saves part of that cost, which is why the central bank wants interoperable, low-cost rails.

Who pays for digital: users. bKash charges Tk 18.50 per Tk 1,000 to cash out at a regular agent — 1.85% — and Tk 13.95 at a ‘Priyo Agent’ for up to Tk 50,000 a month 43; Bangladesh Bank officials say there is no fixed cap on MFS charges 42. Transparency International Bangladesh estimated users paid Tk 44.1–102 billion in cash-out fees in 2024 42 — a sizeable sum next to the Tk 200 billion cost of cash management. By contrast, cross-platform transfers through the national switch are capped at 0.85% from MFS accounts 50. GovTec calculation: the regular cash-out fee is more than twice the cross-platform cap.

Who gains: migrants’ families and safety-net recipients. Remittances through MFS almost doubled to Tk 20,236 crore in 2025 11. In FY2025–26, 40.05% of the Tk 47,597 crore social assistance budget was allocated to digital transfers 44 — about Tk 19,060 crore (GovTec calculation). The World Bank’s $200 million social protection project, approved in March 2025, aims to reach at least 3 million poor people, half of them women 49.

Value for money: the refinance scheme is small (Tk 1,500 crore 10) next to monthly MFS flows above Tk 2 lakh crore 53; its value is in steering credit to women and small borrowers. The largest cost-benefit lever is the phone: a smartphone at Tk 2,000–3,000 instead of about Tk 9,000 46 would open app-based services to far more users, but only if higher import taxes do not offset the subsidy 45. GovTec’s view is that the phone, fee and interoperability decisions matter more than new account drives.

What to watch

  • Publication of the full National Financial Inclusion Survey 2025 report and its definitions, and whether Bangladesh Bank adopts it as the baseline for a successor strategy 15.
  • A new national financial inclusion strategy after NFIS-B 2021–2026, and whether it sets targets for active use and women’s phone ownership 277.
  • Whether bKash and Nagad connect fully to the national switch and IIPS launches by July 2027 519.
  • Final rules for non-bank e-money issuers 23.
  • The final 2026–27 duty on imported phones and the roll-out of low-cost smartphones 4546.
  • Any regulatory cap or guidance on MFS cash-out fees 42.
