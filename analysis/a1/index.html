One switch for every taka: can Bangladesh catch up on interoperable payments?

2 Nov 2026 · Updated 28 Nov 2026 · 16 min read · 38 sources

From today, eight West African countries route all mobile money transfers through one central bank switch. India and Brazil did this years ago. Bangladesh has tried several times since 2020; this year a compulsory Bangla QR finally took off, and the central bank aims for full interoperability by July 2027. What the laws, the money, the people and the comparisons show.

Key numbers

  • 25.47 bn UPI payments in India in October 20261
  • 7.89 bn Pix payments in Brazil in September 20262
  • 38 m users on West Africa’s PI-SPI, a year after launch4
  • Tk 201,337 cr monthly MFS value in Bangladesh, April 202611
  • Tk 143.54 cr daily Bangla QR payments, up from about Tk 28 cr in June 202618

The short version

Interoperability means a person can pay or send money from any bank, wallet or mobile money account to any other, instantly and cheaply. Countries that achieved it did so with a public switch run or backed by the central bank, a deadline that obliged every provider to join, and transfers that are free or nearly free for individuals.

Bangladesh has a large mobile money market: about 237 million registered MFS accounts at the end of 2024, of which 37.6% were active 23, and monthly MFS value that doubled between July 2023 and April 2026 11. Money still moves mostly inside closed networks, but 2026 changed the picture. Bangla QR became compulsory for every merchant on 1 July 16, its daily value rose about fivefold in three months 18, and on 1 November person-to-person payments by QR went live 19. Governor Md Mostaqur Rahman said in October that charges for interoperable Bangla QR transactions were waived regardless of amount, that average daily volume had risen from Tk 37 crore in the first week of June to about Tk 180 crore in the last week of September, and that about Tk 3,000 crore a day would signal more meaningful adoption 38. The central bank’s target is that every bank, MFS provider and other financial institution is on an interoperable system by July 2027 9.

What the leaders did

India’s UPI processed 25.47 billion payments worth ₹31.53 lakh crore in October 2026, about 822 million a day 1. Brazil’s Pix handled 7.89 billion payments worth R$3.81 trillion in September 2026, up 15% in a year 2, and accounted for 55.2% of all payment transactions in Brazil in the first half of 2026 3. Both are free for individuals: NPCI says bank-account UPI payments remain free for customers 13, and Brazil’s central bank made Pix free for individuals from launch 14.

West Africa is the newest case. The BCEAO launched PI-SPI on 30 September 2025 with 45 institutions 5. By October 2026 it connected more than 175 institutions and over 38 million users, and from 2 November 2026 all interoperable person-to-person transfers in the eight WAEMU countries must run through it 4. Transfers are free up to CFAF 8,000 a day, with fees capped at 0.8% above that 6.

Where Bangladesh stands

Bangladesh’s caps on cross-provider transfers are low for bank-originated transfers (0.15%) but high for MFS-originated ones (0.85%, including VAT) 7, which is where most low-income users sit. A sender moving Tk 1,000 from one MFS account to another pays Tk 8.50; in India or Brazil the same transfer is free 71314. Taking money out is costlier still: MFS cash-out fees run from Tk 12.90 to Tk 18.50 per Tk 1,000 26.

The country has also been here before. An NPSB interoperability service launched in 2020 was suspended within hours 7. The Binimoy platform, built for Tk 65 crore and launched in November 2022, attracted only eight banks, three MFS providers and two PSPs before Bangladesh Bank ended it in 2025, citing irregularities and a breach of the agreement behind it 8. The lesson from both is that technology alone does not create interoperability: the incentives of the largest providers and the pricing for users decide whether people actually switch.

Bangla QR: the part that is working

Bangladesh Bank issued the national QR standard, based on the EMV merchant-presented specification, in January 2021 15, and piloted it in Motijheel, Dhaka, from 18 January 2023 28. Adoption stayed voluntary and slow: by the end of 2025 about 9.63 lakh merchants used it, through 46 banks, seven MFS providers and four PSPs 28. On 1 April 2026 the central bank ordered all providers to replace their own QR codes by 30 June, with penalties of up to Tk 30 lakh 20; from 1 July one sticker accepts payment from any bank or wallet app 16.

Use jumped. About 39 lakh merchant points now accept Bangla QR, and daily payments rose from about 1 lakh worth Tk 28 crore in June 2026 to nearly 3.5 lakh worth Tk 143.54 crore by late September 18. Instant crediting of Bangla QR payments had applied to small and marginal merchants since 15 December 2025 30. From 1 October 2026 the central bank extended immediate crediting to all Bangla QR payments, removed the 1% minimum merchant fee and set the interchange fee to zero 1731; it also told providers that by 31 October every savings and current account holder must be able to pay by scanning Bangla QR in their app 31. On 1 November it opened person-to-person transfers by Bangla QR: a user scans another person’s code to send money without typing an account number 1921. Fees and limits for these transfers had not been published at launch 19.

The fast take-up has side effects. Some merchants have started letting customers withdraw cash through Bangla QR without buying anything, avoiding MFS cash-out fees and undercutting about 2 million agents; in one case Tk 36.4 lakh moved through two banks’ QR codes in five days 26.

GovTec’s assessment

Bangladesh has the ingredients that India and Brazil had: a central bank willing to own the switch, open-source infrastructure (Mojaloop), a payments law since 2024 22 and a deep mobile money base. Bangla QR has now proved the method: a fixed deadline, penalties and lower merchant costs moved the market in three months where years of voluntary adoption had not 161828. Two things will decide whether the July 2027 target changes behaviour. First, pricing: while sending money from an MFS account costs up to 0.85% 7 and cash-out up to about 1.85% 24, closed networks keep their advantage, so person-to-person QR transfers need to be free or close to free for small amounts, as the BCEAO now does. Second, controls: cash-out through merchants shows that removing fees without limits creates new channels for unrecorded money, so per-merchant limits and monitoring should come with each step. Publishing monthly interoperability data would let everyone see whether it is working.

Money and value

Scale. Mobile financial services moved about Tk 2 lakh crore a month by April 2026 11. Bangla QR, at Tk 143.54 crore a day 18, is roughly Tk 4,300 crore a month (GovTec calculation), or about 2% of MFS value: fast-growing, but still a small share of digital money flows.

Who pays. Under the old 1% minimum merchant fee, a grocery taking Tk 10,000 a day lost about Tk 3,000 a month 27, and consumer groups asked for a cap of 0.25% 25. Since October the merchant fee floor and interchange are gone; Bangladesh Bank now pays incentives of 0.10% to acquirers and 0.20% to issuers on payments up to Tk 2,000 17, backed by a Tk 100 crore fund 29. For users, an MFS-to-MFS transfer of Tk 1,000 costs Tk 8.50 7, and in an iDEA survey 90.9% of bKash users rated cash-out charges high or very high 24.

Value for money. Binimoy cost Tk 65 crore and was closed with low use 8. Bangla QR, with a modest subsidy fund and a mandate, multiplied daily value about five times in three months 1829. If wider digital payments cut even a tenth of the Tk 20,000 crore yearly cash-handling cost 10, the saving would be about Tk 2,000 crore a year (GovTec illustration), many times the public money spent so far. The main economic risk is leakage: merchant cash-outs move money outside agent controls and could feed informal hundi channels 26, at a time when remittances are about 6% of GDP 10.

What to watch

  • The transaction limits Bangladesh Bank sets for person-to-person Bangla QR transfers, and what the existing interbank transfer charges mean in practice compared with the 0.85% cap on MFS-originated transfers.
  • Limits on merchant cash-outs through Bangla QR, and whether they slow genuine merchant adoption 26.
  • Whether the largest MFS providers are required to route person-to-person transfers through IIPS, and by when.
  • Monthly interoperable transaction data: Bangladesh Bank does not yet publish it separately, which makes progress hard to track.
  • Kenya’s National Payment System Bill, the next big mobile money market to legislate interoperability 12.
