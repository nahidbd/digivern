Paying shops to say yes to QR: can Pakistan’s cashless push beat its cash habit?

14 Nov 2026 · 15 min read · 53 sources

Pakistan has decided to keep paying the fees on Raast QR payments, after the number of shops accepting digital payments passed two million and the Prime Minister ordered a roadmap to “100%” digital payments. Raast is growing fast, but cash in circulation grew faster still. What the policies, the money, the traders and the comparisons with India, Bangladesh and Indonesia show.

Key numbers

  • 742.1 m Raast payments in Jan–Mar 2026, up from 295.7 m a year and a half earlier5
  • 2.03 m merchants actively accepting digital payments, up from 0.5 m22
  • Rs3.5 bn yearly ceiling of the Raast QR merchant-fee subsidy, Sep 2025–Jun 202614
  • Rs12.07 trn currency in circulation, March 2026 — up 28.4% in a year32
  • ~40% digital share of retail payment value, Jan–Mar 2026, against 92% by number (GovTec calculation)5

The short version

On 13 November 2026 Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif decided to continue the subsidy that covers merchant fees on Raast QR payments, “to maintain momentum in digital payments adoption”, Minister of State for Finance Bilal Azhar Kayani said 2. A day earlier Kayani told the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP) to prepare a working paper for a Raast QR merchant discount rate (MDR) scheme, and heard that active merchants accepting digital payments had risen from 0.5 million to 2.03 million 1. The week before, the Prime Minister ordered a roadmap with time-bound targets for “100%” digital payments 43.

By number of payments, Pakistan is already mostly digital: 92% of retail payments in January–March 2026 went through digital channels 5. By value it is not: digital channels carried Rs68.3 trillion of Rs168.8 trillion, about 40% (GovTec calculation) 56. And cash in circulation reached Rs12.07 trillion in March 2026, 28.4% more than a year earlier 32. The subsidy buys merchant acceptance; it does not by itself make shops and customers stop using cash.

How Raast got here

The SBP launched Raast, its instant payment system built with Karandaaz and the Gates Foundation, on 11 January 2021 for bulk payments such as salaries and pensions 11. Free person-to-person transfers followed in February 2022, using a mobile number as a Raast ID, with funds credited within 20 seconds and the SBP providing the service to banks at no cost 12. Merchant payments by QR were planned for November 2022, then June 2023, and went live in September 2023 with only six participants 13.

Use has grown quickly since. Quarterly Raast payments rose from 295.7 million in October–December 2024 to 742.1 million worth Rs23.3 trillion in January–March 2026 75. Person-to-person transfers still dominate: 664 million in that quarter, against 55.9 million merchant payments, although merchant payments grew by more than half in one quarter 5. In FY2025 digital channels handled 88% of retail transactions, up from 85% in FY2024 and 78% in FY2023 89.

The merchant subsidy came in 2025. Under SBP Circular 03 of 2025, the government pays the lower of 0.5% of each Raast QR payment or Rs100, split equally between the merchant’s and the customer’s institutions, up to Rs3.5 billion for September 2025 to June 2026; institutions may still charge merchants up to 0.25% for onboarding and servicing 14. The money came through a technical supplementary grant approved by the Economic Coordination Committee, which asked the SBP for an impact report by July 2026 1516.

What other countries did

India removed MDR on UPI merchant payments from January 2020 and has since paid banks an incentive instead: 0.15% on small-merchant payments of up to ₹2,000 in FY2024-25, with an outlay of ₹1,500 crore 37, and ₹2,000 crore budgeted for UPI and RuPay incentives in FY2026-27 38. That model is now changing. India notified a 0.4% MDR on merchant payments above ₹2,000 in September 2026 39, though its start has been deferred and no date had been announced by 11 November 40, and NPCI’s new UPI Vyapar Saksham scheme will pay acquiring banks ₹300 per QR code and ₹750 per soundbox from a share of MDR income 41. Merchant payments are now 63.2% of UPI’s 25.47 billion monthly transactions 42.

Indonesia charges micro merchants 0% on QRIS payments up to Rp500,000 and 0.3% above, and from 1 October 2026 extended 0% to all merchants for payments up to Rp100,000 4647. Bangladesh made Bangla QR compulsory for all merchants from July 2026, scrapped its 1% minimum merchant fee from October, and set up a Tk100 crore fund with the IFC to support small merchants 484950. Sri Lanka waived QR fees for small merchants on payments up to Rs5,000 in April 2026 51. Brazil took another route: Pix is free for individuals by central bank rule, while firms can be charged 45; Pix reached 55.2% of all payment transactions in the first half of 2026 44.

The research is cautious about subsidies. A BIS working paper finds that zero-fee fast payment systems are hard to sustain without outside funding or other revenue 52, and a World Bank review found that subsidies expand acceptance points but more terminals or QR codes do not guarantee more electronic payments 53.

Where Pakistan stands

Against the targets set in August 2025, FY2026 was a mixed year: active digital merchants passed the 2 million target and mobile banking users reached 137 million against a target of 120 million, but digital transactions reached 11.9 billion against a target of 15 billion 202122. Merchant counts depend on the definition: more than 2.6 million merchants had been onboarded to Raast merchant payments by March 2026 7, while 2.03 million were active 22, up from fewer than 700,000 retailers linked to digital payments in November 2025 19. The Pakistan Bureau of Statistics counts more than 2.7 million retail units alone 25.

Government payments are the next lever. The SBP planned to route all government payments through Raast by the end of FY2026 17; that slipped, and cheques for Asaan Assignment Accounts end only on 15 November 2026, with Raast taking over from 16 November 13. BISP cash transfers already go to digital wallets 3. The SBP also capped card fees at fuel stations at Re1 a litre until January 2027 and told banks to enable Raast QR there 18.

Budgets have mostly used tax on cash rather than rewards for digital payments. The FY2025-26 budget raised withholding tax on cash withdrawals by non-filers from 0.6% to 1% 31; the FY2026-27 budget proposed cutting tax on card spending at foreign merchants from 5% to 0.5% 30. Financial inclusion reached 69% in 2025 against a 2028 target of 75%, with a gender gap of 29% 29.

The merchant problem

Shopkeepers’ hesitation is less about technology than about records. Atiq Mir of the All Karachi Tajir Ittehad says merchants remain hesitant about Raast QR because of confidentiality and income concerns 25. When Punjab’s revenue authority banned cash payments at salons, hotels and restaurants in February 2026, the hotel and restaurant association asked for six months of parallel cash and digital payments, saying many customers had no wallets 26. A columnist summed up the trader view: “a paper trail means taxes” 28.

Fraud is the other brake. Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb said cybersecurity and fraud prevention “must remain central” to the push 34, and the SBP opened a national survey of digital payment users in June 2026 covering reliability, complaints and fraud 36. The government is also developing a payment system for areas without internet access 4.

GovTec’s assessment

This is GovTec’s view. Pakistan has built the infrastructure: Raast is free for people, fast, owned by the central bank and already carries most payments by number 125. The subsidy has helped turn merchant registration into acceptance, and Rs3.5 billion a year is small next to the Rs21 billion that printing banknotes cost in FY2023 1413.

But three things limit what the subsidy can do. First, it is temporary and open-ended at the same time: the circular ran to June 2026, the continuation announced in November has no published amount or end date, and the impact report the ECC asked for has not been published 14216. Second, merchants’ main cost is not the fee but the tax visibility that comes with digital records; India and Bangladesh paired zero or low fees with mandates, and India is now moving to charge larger merchants to fund small ones 394149. Third, the measure that matters most — cash in the economy — is still rising 32.

A credible roadmap would publish the subsidy’s cost, length and results; set a target for cash in circulation or the digital share by value, not only by number; pair mandatory acceptance with simple, predictable tax treatment for small traders; and put government payments on Raast on the dates already promised.

Money and value

Who pays: the federal budget, through a technical supplementary grant, pays up to 0.5% of each Raast QR payment (capped at Rs100) to the two institutions involved 1415. The Rs3.5 billion ceiling matches 0.5% of the Rs700 billion of merchant payments the government expected to cover (GovTec calculation) 15. Merchants may still pay up to 0.25%, so acceptance is cheap but not necessarily free 14.

Who gains: banks and wallets receive income on payments that would otherwise earn nothing, which is why Kayani asked them to do more in return 2. Merchants gain cheaper acceptance than cards, which cost 1.5–2.5% under the 2020 framework 13. The state gains if cash handling falls: the subsidy ceiling is about one-sixth of the FY2023 cost of printing banknotes (GovTec calculation) 1413, and digital records widen the tax base.

Value for money is not yet shown. Merchant payments grew from 36.3 million to 55.9 million in one quarter 5, but currency in circulation rose 28.4% in the year to March 2026 32. Without the SBP’s impact report, the public cannot tell how much of the growth the subsidy caused. Outside funding is modest: the Gates Foundation committed US$13.7 million to Karandaaz in December 2025 for Raast oversight, wider use and links with six Gulf countries 23.

What to watch

  • The amount, length and conditions of the continued Raast QR subsidy, and whether the SBP working paper turns it into a standing MDR scheme 12.
  • Publication of the SBP’s impact report and the third-party validation of cashless targets 1621.
  • Whether assignment-account payments move to Raast smoothly from 16 November, and whether departments are fully digital by December 13.
  • Currency in circulation and the digital share by value in the next SBP payment systems review 325.
  • The law on compulsory digital acceptance being drafted, and any offline payment option for areas without internet 274.
  • When India’s 0.4% UPI merchant fee takes effect, a test of whether larger merchants can fund small ones 4041.
