Singapore and Germany's central banks settle euro–Singapore dollar trades in wholesale CBDC across two unlinked ledgers in Project Simbolon test

16 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) and the Deutsche Bundesbank said on 16 November that they had successfully tested connections between their test environments for settling digital assets. Under Project Simbolon, euro/Singapore dollar foreign exchange trades were settled payment-versus-payment in wholesale central bank digital currencies, so that neither leg completes without the other. Zero-knowledge proofs let each side confirm that settlement conditions were met without directly integrating the two central bank ledgers. DBS and Deutsche Bank took part as commercial banks, with Partior as technology provider. The test is the first major milestone under a memorandum the two central banks signed in November 2025. They published an insights note and plan a fuller report. FinanceFeeds notes it was a controlled test, not a live payment corridor, and no launch date or cost data were given.

Why it matters

It shows a way for central banks to remove settlement risk in cross-border FX without merging their systems, an approach relevant to any country planning to connect its payment infrastructure across borders.
