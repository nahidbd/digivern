Nigeria’s $500 million World Bank farm loan pays states only if they set up rules for a common digital farmer registry and other reforms

23 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Details of the financing agreement for Nigeria's Sustainable Agricultural Value-Chains for Growth (AGROW) project, reported on 23 November 2026, show that the $500 million International Development Association credit is tied to reforms by participating states. Finance Minister Taiwo Oyedele signed on 29 October 2026 and World Bank Country Director Mathew Verghis on 16 September. States must issue regulations for a common digital farmer registry, adopt a responsible land investment framework and publish inter-state trade fees. The registry condition carries $16 million, capped at $1 million per state, with targets of 40% basic and 70% enhanced coverage. The agreement also requires risk assessments before AI tools are deployed and bars uses such as mass surveillance and social scoring. Repayment runs from May 2031 to November 2050.

Why it matters

It makes a shared digital farmer registry, and safeguards on AI use, a condition of agricultural financing for Nigerian states.
