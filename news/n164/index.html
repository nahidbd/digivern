US financial intelligence unit FinCEN takes some systems offline after unauthorised access and extends filing deadlines

24–25 Nov 2026 · 1 source · media sourcing

The US Treasury’s Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN), which collects the suspicious-activity and other reports banks file under the Bank Secrecy Act, said it is investigating a cybersecurity incident that resulted in unauthorised access to a limited subset of its systems, and that it has taken some systems offline as a precaution. The BSA E-Filing System remains operational but some services may be temporarily unavailable. In an order dated 24 November 2026 FinCEN granted temporary, limited exceptive relief, extending deadlines for filers affected by the disruption. It has not said who was behind the incident or whether any data was accessed or taken. The incident follows reported breaches at other federal agencies, including an apparent hack of an FBI national law-enforcement database under investigation.

Why it matters

FinCEN holds some of the most sensitive financial data any government keeps; an intrusion into the system that receives banks’ reports shows why financial intelligence units need the same protection as payment infrastructure.
