World Bank policy note tells governments to build digital wallet ecosystems on open standards rather than single government apps

9 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

A new World Bank policy note, the third in its series on digital wallets, sets out principles for national digital wallets. It argues that a wallet is not simply a government smartphone app but an ecosystem linking independent credential issuers, verifiers, wallet providers and trust service providers, and that governments should map those roles, legal mandates and trust governance before buying anything. It recommends adopting open standards early (W3C Verifiable Credentials, OpenID4VC and ISO 18013-5) to allow cross-border use and avoid vendor lock-in, citing the EU Digital Identity Wallet as an example at national scale. It advises against large, rigid IT contracts, favours reusing existing portals, ID databases and public key infrastructure, and proposes phased roll-outs from discovery and proof of concept to pilot and live service, with a government product owner and attention to inclusion.

Why it matters

Many countries, including Bangladesh with its planned single digital ID, are about to procure wallets; the note gives ministries a checklist to avoid buying a closed app that locks them into one vendor.
