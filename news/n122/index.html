UK peers call the government ‘complacent’ on hostile-state cyber threats and say the cyber bill leaves most of the public sector out

16 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · media sourcing

The House of Lords National Resilience Committee published “Are we ready? Building a culture of resilience” on 16 November. It says the UK is “underprepared” for potentially catastrophic risks, including cyberattacks by Russia, China and Iran, and that the government “appears complacent”. The Record reports that the committee finds the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill useful as a baseline but too narrow, noting that most of the public sector is outside it. The report came eight days before a major vote on the bill. The committee asks for wider cyber regulation, regulators with enough resources to enforce it, and better sharing of threat intelligence with critical-infrastructure operators. Its wider recommendations include a Cabinet minister for resilience supported by a chief resilience officer, an annual preparedness statement from the Prime Minister, and statutory stress-testing in strategic sectors. A Cabinet Office spokesperson said the government would review the report and is already taking the action it calls for.

Why it matters

It adds parliamentary pressure to widen the UK’s main cyber law, including to the public sector, just before a key vote.
