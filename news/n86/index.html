ECOWAS states agree to accept each other's biometric ID cards as travel documents at borders from 1 January 2027

11 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

Heads of immigration of the Economic Community of West African States adopted the Freetown Declaration at their tenth annual meeting in Freetown, Sierra Leone, on 3–4 November 2026. From 1 January 2027, member states must accept a valid ECOWAS National Biometric Identity Card (ENBIC) issued by any member as proof of identity and community citizenship at official crossings, even if the issuing country has not finished its own rollout. Immigration services must put in place procedures, staff training and equipment to verify other states' cards. Seven countries have launched the card so far: Nigeria, Ghana, Senegal, Benin, The Gambia, Guinea-Bissau and Sierra Leone. Members must send rollout plans with target dates to the ECOWAS Commission, which was asked to speed up common technical standards and a regional verification mechanism. An earlier ECOWAS commitment had aimed for acceptance by December 2026.

Why it matters

Mutual recognition turns national ID cards into regional infrastructure for free movement, but it depends on border posts having verification kit, trained staff and connectivity.
