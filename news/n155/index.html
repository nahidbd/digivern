Hong Kong cuts Gleneagles Hospital off its eHealth record-sharing system after hackers reached patient data through the hospital's app

24 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · official sourcing

Hong Kong's Health Bureau has suspended Gleneagles Hospital Hong Kong's registration with eHealth, the territory's electronic health record-sharing system, with effect from 23 November 2026. The private hospital found unauthorised activity on 18 November affecting the security infrastructure behind its My Gleneagles SmartHealth app, which led to unauthorised access to personal data of some patients and other people; it told the Electronic Health Record Office and the Department of Health on 23 November. The suspension, made under section 24 of the Electronic Health System Ordinance (Cap. 625), disconnects the hospital so it can neither view nor upload records through eHealth until officials are satisfied its systems pose no unacceptable security risk. The government says the app has no direct link to eHealth and there is no evidence the central system or its data were compromised. The Department of Health has asked the hospital to notify affected patients quickly, submit a detailed investigation report within four weeks, and says it will assess possible breaches of the Private Healthcare Facilities Ordinance (Cap. 633); no figure for the number of people affected has been given.

Why it matters

It shows how a breach at one provider's patient app can force regulators to isolate that provider from a national health record exchange to protect the wider system.
