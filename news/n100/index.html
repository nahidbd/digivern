African countries adopt the Nairobi Health Data Action Agenda as WHO finds none yet fully able to use health data and evidence

10–14 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

Delegates at the first Africa Health Data Forum, convened by WHO with partners in Nairobi from 10 to 12 November 2026, adopted the Nairobi Health Data Action Agenda. Its eight priorities include national financing for health information systems, training the health data workforce, connecting fragmented information systems, and responsible use of digital tools and AI. Africa Presse reports that 51 countries adopted the agenda on 12 November and that more than 300 participants from WHO’s African and Eastern Mediterranean regions attended. A WHO assessment of health information system maturity in 40 countries gave an overall score of 59 out of 100; Africa Presse adds that only 18% of the 40 had a dedicated budget line and that 19 of them notify authorities of at least 80% of deaths. A second WHO report, based on the SCORE 2025 assessment of 52 countries, found that none had reached the highest level of sustainable capacity to use data and evidence. Ethiopia, Rwanda, Kenya and Egypt presented their experiences. Progress will be tracked under a continental framework to 2030 with annual national reviews, and the forum will meet every two years, next in 2028. Dr Abdurahmane Diallo, WHO Africa’s Director of Programme Management, said the agenda must translate countries’ priorities into practical investments.

Why it matters

Most African health information systems still depend on donor projects and disconnected tools; a shared agenda with annual reviews gives ministries a yardstick for financing and joining them up, including the digital health records and AI tools now being rolled out.
