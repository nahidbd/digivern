UN weapons treaty talks end without a mandate to negotiate a ban on autonomous weapons, giving experts until 2028 instead

21–23 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

The Seventh Review Conference of the UN Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons closed in Geneva in the early hours of Saturday 21 November 2026 without agreeing a mandate to negotiate a legally binding treaty on lethal autonomous weapons systems. The convention works by consensus, and opposition from major military powers, including the United States and Russia, blocked formal negotiations among the 128 states parties. Instead, the conference gave the Group of Governmental Experts a two-year mandate for 2027–2028 to develop an international instrument, leaving open whether it will be binding. Nearly 100 states had backed negotiations; the Stop Killer Robots coalition counted 98. The Swiss presidency called the compromise a significant step forward, while campaigners called it a 'lowest common denominator' outcome.

Why it matters

It means there will be no binding global rules on AI-enabled weapons before 2028 at the earliest, after more than a decade of talks.
