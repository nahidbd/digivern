UK financial regulators consult on cyber resilience expectations for firms, with extra guidance on AI and quantum risks

19 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

The Prudential Regulation Authority, the Bank of England and the Financial Conduct Authority published a joint consultation (CP17/26 and GC26/9) on cyber risk and resilience management on 19 November 2026. It sets principles-based, outcome-focused expectations rather than new rules, explaining how firms should meet existing requirements on governance, operational resilience, operational risk and third-party risk in a cyber context. Topics include senior management accountability, critical IT infrastructure, identity and access management, data protection and supply-chain weaknesses, and firms are expected to be able to rebuild critical systems from scratch because backups may be compromised. Alongside it, the regulators published two non-binding ‘effective practices’ papers on cyber risk relating to artificial intelligence and to quantum computing. The FCA received 38 cyber incidents in October 2026, the second-highest monthly total in five years. Responses are due by 26 February 2027.

Why it matters

It shows a major financial centre tightening cyber expectations for banks and payment firms as AI shortens the time between a flaw being found and exploited.
