Poland takes its ePUAP e-government platform offline after a cyberattack

10–14 Nov 2026 · 4 sources · media sourcing

Poland temporarily shut down ePUAP, the nationwide platform for submitting official documents and completing administrative procedures online, after a cyberattack on 10 November 2026. Digital Affairs Minister Krzysztof Gawkowski said on social media that the attack had been detected and stopped, and that the infrastructure of the Central Information Technology Centre (COI) remained secure. Ministry spokesperson Marek Gieorgica told the Polish Press Agency that the system had been disabled as a precaution. ePUAP electronic mailboxes used by citizens and public offices were unavailable, and the platform was still offline on 14 November. Gov.pl warned that the Trusted Profile (Profil Zaufany), the national electronic identification node and signature services might be unavailable from 6 p.m. on 14 November to 7 a.m. on 15 November for maintenance. On 12 November Gawkowski said the attack had come from abroad and that logs showed preparations for an operation meant to paralyse public administration in December; the Internal Security Agency (ABW) is investigating. Officials had not said how the attack was carried out or whether any data were taken.

Why it matters

ePUAP and the Trusted Profile are the backbone of Polish e-government; after attacks on Belgium’s tax portal, Moldova’s e-services and the Netherlands’ DigiD, the outage shows how shared login and mailbox platforms have become prime targets.
