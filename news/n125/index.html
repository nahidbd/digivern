California proposes licensed independent verifiers, emergency shutdown orders and 48-hour incident reporting for frontier AI developers

18 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

California Governor Gavin Newsom on 18 November 2026 released legislative recommendations based on the Government Operations Agency report “AI Safety in 2026 and Beyond”, presenting them at a roundtable in Washington. Large frontier developers would be overseen by licensed independent verification organizations, and state officials could order the shutdown of a model posing an imminent catastrophic risk if the developer refuses to contain it. The report proposes cutting the deadline for most critical incident reports from 15 days to 48 hours, and would let officials impose a time-limited cap on automated AI research (recursive self-improvement) under defined conditions. It responds to Newsom’s 18 September 2026 executive order and was developed with outside advisers Jason Goldman, Gillian Hadfield, Alondra Nelson and Rob Reich. The recommendations are not law; lawmakers are expected to take them up in December, and Newsom leaves office in January.

Why it matters

It is the most detailed government blueprint yet for continuous, state-run oversight of frontier AI models, and Newsom pitches it as a template for federal rules.
