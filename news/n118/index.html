Uganda’s National Medical Stores asks Parliament to fund electronic records to track medicines down to health-centre level

17 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

The general manager of Uganda’s National Medical Stores (NMS), Dr Moses Kamabare, told Parliament’s Committee on Health on 17 November that lower-level health facilities cannot properly account for the medicines NMS supplies because they have no digitised drug-monitoring system, which hampers efforts against theft and unexplained stock-outs. According to Parliament’s press release, he said the Ministry of Health and partners are pursuing an electronic medical records system to trace who receives medicine at public facilities, but that “funds have not been released for this”. He asked that referral hospitals be able to account for every item they receive by the end of the financial year. Committee chair Dr Julius Monday Rude said close to 95% of referral hospitals report digitising systems and installing CCTV, while MP Stephenson Lokoru said health centres have not followed. The ministry introduced electronic medical records in 2018, but most facilities are still not covered. In October, Health Minister Chris Baryomunsi said the ministry had finalised plans to digitise the movement of medicines from suppliers to facilities and patients, with a dashboard to trace drugs at each stage, speaking at the launch of a supply chain project at Joint Medical Stores in Kampala, the Daily Monitor reported.

Why it matters

It shows that Uganda’s health records rollout, started in 2018, still stops at referral hospitals because of unreleased funds, leaving the medicine supply chain hard to audit.
