Germany’s Bundesrat approves the digital identities law, clearing the way for the ‘d-you’ wallet on 2 January 2027

20 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · official sourcing

Germany’s upper house, the Bundesrat, approved the Digital Identities Act on Friday 20 November, ID Tech reported, completing parliamentary passage of the legal basis for the country’s European Digital Identity Wallet, called d-you. The Bundestag had passed the law (Digitale Identitätengesetz) on 15 October with the votes of the CDU/CSU and SPD; the Greens, the Left and AfD voted against. The wallet is due from 2 January 2027 and will be free and voluntary, with non-digital routes kept. Early uses include proving identity and age, opening bank accounts and signing contracts, with a digital driving licence also planned from 2 January. About 40 partners are involved and more than 300 use cases are being prepared, around 50 of them for launch, Borncity reported. Users will need an NFC phone running Android 14 or iOS 26.4 and their ID card or residence permit with its six-digit PIN. Committee changes in the Bundestag added a right to refuse inclusion of the biometric photo and a call for a pseudonym function as early as possible. The 2027 federal budget allocates €155 million to the wallet, according to Borncity.

Why it matters

Germany is the EU’s largest economy and its wallet launch is a major test of the EU digital identity framework ahead of the 2026–27 deadlines.
