Africa’s instant payment systems handled 71.4 billion transactions worth $1.8 trillion in 2025, but most users still rely heavily on cash

19 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · expert sourcing

The AfricaNenda Foundation launched the fifth State of Inclusive Instant Payment Systems in Africa (SIIPS) report in Mauritius on 19 November 2026, produced with the UN Economic Commission for Africa (UNECA) and, according to The Sun, the World Bank Group. It counts 71.4 billion instant payment transactions worth $1.8 trillion in 2025, 11% more transactions than in 2024 and nearly triple the 26.4 billion recorded in 2021. There are 39 live instant payment systems — 35 domestic and four regional — serving 43 African countries as of July 2026, up from 29 systems in 2022; four went live between July 2025 and July 2026. Nigeria’s NIP remains the only system rated ‘mature’ on AfricaNenda’s inclusivity scale. A survey of 1,403 individual and small-business users in 15 markets, fielded in March–April 2026, found 55% still relied on cash for about half of their transactions, and 43% of users who lost money in a digital payment cut back their use. Lead author Sabine Mensah, AfricaNenda’s deputy CEO, said instant payment systems can deepen inclusion through greater use of digital payments.

Why it matters

The rails are now in place across most of Africa; the report shows the next problem is trust and everyday use, which is also where Bangladesh, Pakistan and other countries building inclusive switches are struggling.
