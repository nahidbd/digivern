Kosovo launches a national eID, digital wallet and e-signature service, with enrolment from 9 November

3–5 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Kosovo’s Ministry of Digitalization and Public Administration unveiled a national electronic identity platform, a digital wallet app for Android and iOS and an electronic signature service in Prishtina on 3 November. Enrolment starts on 9 November at seven regional centres of the Civil Registration Agency. The eID becomes the login for platforms such as eKosova, which already offers more than 330 digital services, replacing usernames and passwords; businesses can authenticate documents with electronic seals, and business registration is the first process to use e-signatures. Integrated service centres, starting in Prishtina, will help people who cannot use digital channels alone. The EU funded the project with €3.2 million through its Instrument for Pre-Accession Assistance, and the World Bank and Japan have backed the reforms since 2022. Recognition across borders under the EU digital identity (EUDI) framework is a longer-term aim that depends on closing gaps the World Bank has identified, and it is not yet clear how citizens abroad will obtain credentials.

Why it matters

Kosovo is building its wallet with EU alignment in mind from the start, a test of whether Western Balkan states can connect to the EUDI ecosystem ahead of EU membership.
