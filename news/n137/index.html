Vietnam passes a law requiring banking, telecom and social media accounts to be linked to a national electronic identity

20 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Vietnam’s National Assembly has passed the Law on Electronic Identification and Authentication, ID Tech reported on 20 November, with 473 deputies (94.79%) voting in favour. The law takes effect on 1 July 2027. It requires online accounts in sectors including banking, telecommunications, securities, e-commerce, e-wallets, digital assets, online games and social networks to be verified against, and linked to, a national electronic identity; ministers can add other high-risk transactions in their sectors. VNeID becomes the official app for identification, authentication and public services, and notices sent through it will carry the same legal weight as paper ones. The law bans buying, selling or renting electronic identities and using fake biometrics or AI-generated images to pass checks. Organisations may not refuse access after a single failed check without a review route, and agencies may not ask for documents that already exist in verified electronic form. Use of identity data containing personal information needs the person’s consent. The state identification authority is due to start issuing public digital signature certificates from 1 March 2027. The National Assembly’s newspaper reported in early November that the draft was being revised for adoption at the end of the session.

Why it matters

It makes a single state digital identity the gateway to almost every online account, with privacy and surveillance implications.
