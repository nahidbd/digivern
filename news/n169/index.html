Sri Lanka’s state fuel company says automation and an AI dispatch queue helped cut its staff from 3,292 to 2,031

27 Nov 2026 · 1 source · media sourcing

Ceylon Petroleum Corporation (CPC), Sri Lanka’s state-owned fuel company, has reduced its headcount from 3,292 in 2024 to 2,031 by automating office administration and fuel distribution, its managing director Dr Mayura Neththikumarage told the WION Digital Sri Lanka Summit 2026 in Colombo, EconomyNext reported on 27 November. Filling-station owners now order and pay through a mobile app linked to CPC’s SAP enterprise system, and orders are processed daily at 6:00 a.m.; the company stopped printing invoices about three months ago. AI tools have replaced manual typing and translation, and an automated AI queue now schedules fuel bowsers instead of staff. “Nobody who is powerful can talk to somebody and get this change because it is AI,” he said. CPC first adopted SAP in 2010 and is upgrading to the cloud-based SAP S/4HANA RISE. The report did not say whether the reduction came through layoffs or attrition.

Why it matters

It is a rare quantified case of a South Asian state enterprise using automation and AI both to cut costs and to remove room for political interference in who gets served first — with clear implications for public-sector jobs.
