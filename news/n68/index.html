Papua New Guinea’s “voluntary” SevisPass digital ID is being required for more and more services

3 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

Papua New Guinea’s National Digital Identity Policy 2025 says no one will be denied essential services for lack of a digital ID, and the official SevisPNG FAQ says SevisPass is not mandatory but may be required for certain government services. A review published by Biometric Update on 3 November 2026 lists a growing number of uses that require it: proposed SIM registration rules would oblige adult mobile subscribers to obtain a SevisPass and link their numbers; the ICT minister announced in April 2026 that digital ID would be mandatory for businesses and individuals in state tenders; the Department of Personnel Management has told agencies to prepare for the enrolment of all public servants by 31 December 2026; and the National Social Media Policy 2025 envisages SevisPass checks for access to social media, with no timetable yet. The Department of ICT and the Electoral Commission are also preparing biometric electoral enrolment through the SevisWallet app, with a first campaign proposed from 13 December 2026 to 12 February 2027. ICT Secretary Steven Matainaho has said SevisPass does not automatically register a person to vote. The number of SevisPass holders has not been published.

Why it matters

When a voluntary ID becomes the key to phones, jobs, tenders and public services, it is mandatory in practice; with about a quarter of people online at the end of 2025, safeguards for those left out matter.
