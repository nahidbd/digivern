South Sudan’s central bank and telecom regulator sign a cybersecurity pact for digital financial services

4–6 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

The Bank of South Sudan and the National Communication Authority (NCA) signed a memorandum of understanding covering cybersecurity, information technology, information sharing and business continuity for digital financial services, Sudans Post reported on 6 November 2026. Speaking at the signing, Governor Addis Ababa Othow said cybersecurity is now a question of financial stability, governance and national security, not only an ICT matter, and that the central bank’s duties extend to the integrity and resilience of financial infrastructure. NCA Director General Gieth Kon Mathiang said the authority has worked with the ITU since 2024 on the security of digital financial services, training its officers, testing mobile financial apps and helping to draft security guidelines. He called for more investment, including a dedicated cybersecurity laboratory and technical equipment, and for public awareness of fraud and account protection. The two bodies said they would now turn the agreement into practical measures; the text of the MoU has not been published.

Why it matters

As mobile money spreads in one of the world’s least digitally developed states (192nd of 193 in the UN E-Government Development Index 2024), joint oversight by the central bank and the telecom regulator is the first line of defence against fraud and attacks on payment services.
