Ghana’s 2027 budget sets aside US$300 million for a national AI centre and upgrading rural 2G networks to 4G

12 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · media sourcing

Ghana’s Finance Minister, Cassiel Ato Forson, presented the 2027 Budget Statement to Parliament on 12 November 2026. It commits US$300 million over 2027–2028 to set up a National Artificial Intelligence Centre and to upgrade rural 2G mobile networks to 4G. The money is the ‘technology economy’ pillar, the smallest of eight, in the government’s US$10 billion New Economy programme, which also gives US$3 billion to commercial agriculture and US$2.2 billion to energy infrastructure. The AI centre is to offer shared high-performance computing, secure data environments, applied research labs, model testing, training and start-up support, with priority sectors including agriculture, health, energy, mining, logistics, Ghanaian languages and public services. The budget promises safeguards on data privacy, cybersecurity, accessibility and human accountability in high-impact decisions. The 4G upgrade is meant to bring weather and market information, digital payments, telehealth and online learning to underserved areas. Reports do not say how the money is split between the two projects or how many sites will be upgraded.

Why it matters

The pledge funds both shared AI computing and basic rural connectivity; without a split or a site count it will be hard to track whether the money reaches rural areas.
