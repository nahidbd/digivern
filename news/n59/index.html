Canada’s Budget 2026 creates Digital Transformation Canada to lead AI adoption in the public service, with a fund of about $1 billion

5 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

Canada’s Budget 2026 sets up Digital Transformation Canada, a new federal organisation that will lead the adoption of AI and other emerging technologies across the public service. It brings together Shared Services Canada and the Canadian Digital Service, will recruit private-sector experts through a fellowship programme ($17.6 million over five years from 2026-27, and $4.2 million in 2031-32), and the government intends to introduce a Digital Transformation Canada Act. A Government Transformation Fund of approximately $1 billion over five years, on a cash basis, from 2026-27 is meant to speed up transformative IT projects. The budget points to AI tools already in use: the GCtranslate service is used by over 70 departments, and the Canada Revenue Agency’s AI virtual assistant answered about 2.6 million questions in 1.8 million chat sessions in 2025-26. CTV News reports that the government remains on pace to cut the size of the public service by 10 per cent by 2029.

Why it matters

Canada is pairing a central digital agency and a dedicated fund with a smaller public service; how the new body balances savings targets with service quality and digital sovereignty is a model other governments planning similar consolidations will watch.
