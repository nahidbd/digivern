Samoa launches its National ID Policy 2026, with compulsory biometric registration due to start in 2027

29 Sep – 9 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · official sourcing

Samoa launched its National ID Policy 2026 on 29 September at Taumeasina Island Resort, through the Samoa Bureau of Statistics, with details reported by Biometric Update on 9 November. The policy sets the framework for the National Digital Identification (NDID) system built on a Samoa Digital Identification Number (SDIN) for every registered person. The system has four parts: civil registration and vital statistics, an identity database, a biometric database and authentication services. A one-time national registration campaign is scheduled for 2027: adults aged 18 and over will give face and fingerprint biometrics, children will be registered and given an SDIN, and registration at birth and in the mass campaign is free. Village council and community halls will serve as registration hubs, with portable kits for rural areas and outer islands and home visits for older people and people with disabilities; people who cannot give biometrics can be verified by officials or community leaders, PIN or token, or other documents. Government agencies, banks, utilities and health providers that want to use the system need the Registrar General’s approval. The policy adds no obligations beyond the National Digital Identification Act 2024. The Samoa Bureau of Statistics leads procurement of the system, funded by the World Bank under the Samoa Finance Sector Resilience and Development Project.

Why it matters

Small island states are building national ID from scratch; Samoa’s policy shows how one plans for outer islands, people who cannot give fingerprints and controlled access by banks and agencies before any system is bought.
