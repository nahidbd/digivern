India’s UPI steering committee reportedly settles on a flat ₹5 fee for merchant-to-merchant payments

18–19 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Business Standard reported on 18 November 2026, citing people familiar with the matter, that the National Payments Corporation of India’s UPI and Services Steering Committee has set a flat ₹5 merchant discount rate for merchant-to-merchant (B2B) UPI transfers, such as wholesaler-to-retailer payments, B2B marketplaces and B2B collections. Traders had feared charges of up to ₹300 under a 0.4% rate on payments above ₹2,000; at 0.4%, a typical B2B transfer of ₹20,000–40,000 would have cost ₹80–160. Elets BFSI reported on 19 November that the committee had cleared the ₹5 rate. No rollout date has been set, the wider fee framework planned for 15 October was deferred, and NPCI did not comment. Person-to-person and ordinary person-to-merchant UPI payments are not affected by the reports.

Why it matters

Who pays for UPI is India’s biggest open question in digital payments; a small flat fee for business payments would test whether a zero-price public rail can start recovering costs without pushing traders back to cash.
