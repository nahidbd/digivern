Pakistan orders a roadmap to “100%” digital payments and moves government assignment-account payments from cheques to Raast

6–7 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · media sourcing

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif chaired a high-level meeting on a cashless economy in Islamabad on 6 November 2026 and directed officials to prepare a clear roadmap with time-bound targets for a 100% digital payments system, Dawn reported, citing a statement from the Prime Minister’s Office. He also said internet access must be ensured across the country. The meeting was told that cheque payments through Assignment Accounts will end from 15 November and be replaced by the State Bank of Pakistan’s Raast instant payment system, that drawing and disbursing officers have been trained, that payments in most institutions are to be fully digital by December 2026, and that all payments under the Benazir Income Support Programme already go to digital wallets. A system for areas without internet is being developed. The Prime Minister ordered a media campaign to promote paying electricity and gas bills through the QR codes printed on them. Daily Pakistan reported that the change covers only government Assignment Accounts, not personal or business cheques.

Why it matters

Moving the government’s own payments onto Raast is the fastest way to build volume on a public instant payment system; the harder test is reaching people and places without reliable internet.
