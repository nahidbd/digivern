West Africa makes mobile money interoperable through one instant payment system and doubles wallet limits

2 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

From 2 November 2026, new rules from the Central Bank of West African States (BCEAO) apply across the eight WAEMU countries. Instruction no. 020, signed on 8 October, requires interoperable person-to-person transfers to run through the BCEAO’s PI-SPI instant payment system, which already links more than 175 institutions and over 38 million users. Transfers are free up to CFAF 8,000 a day per user, with fees capped at 0.8% above that, and receiving money stays free. The maximum balance for an identified customer doubles from CFAF 2 million to CFAF 4 million, the monthly top-up ceiling rises from CFAF 10 million to CFAF 15 million, and issuers must keep 65% of customer funds in demand deposits, down from 75%.

Why it matters

A regional central bank is using a public instant payment switch to end closed mobile money networks, one of the clearest cases of payments being run as digital public infrastructure.
