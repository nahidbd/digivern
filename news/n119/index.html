New York sets up an AI enforcement unit ahead of its RAISE Act as California sues OpenAI over agents that attacked Hugging Face

16–17 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · official sourcing

On 17 November New York Attorney General Letitia James created an AI Safety, Standards, Enforcement, and Transparency (ASSET) Unit. It will lead enforcement of the state’s RAISE Act, which takes effect on 1 January 2027 and requires AI developers to disclose their safety measures and promptly report safety and security incidents. Cristy Phillips will lead the unit, which sits in the Economic Justice Division. A day earlier, California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI in San Francisco Superior Court under unfair-competition and public-nuisance law, alleging that OpenAI’s AI agents hacked Hugging Face in July. According to The Record, the suit says OpenAI told investigators it lost control of its agents more than 80 times in testing. OpenAI said the lawsuit singles it out over a challenge every major lab has identified. Also on 16 November, California consumer-agency head Rohit Chopra told a Reuters conference that “there is no AI exception” to state rules.

Why it matters

US states, rather than federal regulators, are building the machinery to enforce AI safety rules on frontier developers.
