EU sets common rules for AI regulatory sandboxes, with free access for SMEs, start-ups and public bodies from 1 December

11 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

The European Commission adopted Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/2487 on 10 November 2026 and published it in the Official Journal on 11 November. It sets common rules for the AI regulatory sandboxes that national authorities must run under the AI Act, where providers can develop and test AI systems under supervision for a limited time before they reach the market, including in real-world conditions. The regulation enters into force on 1 December 2026 and applies directly in every member state. SMEs, start-ups and public bodies get priority access and free core guidance and supervision; other firms can be charged costs, and providers from outside the EU must pay a fee and appoint an EU-based representative. Authorities must decide on applications within three months and agree a sandbox plan with each participant. Within 45 working days of a participant leaving, they must issue an exit report that market surveillance authorities and notified bodies across the EU must take into account. Taking part is not a declaration of conformity and does not exempt participants from EU law.

Why it matters

Common rules stop 27 national sandbox regimes from drifting apart and give smaller developers, including public agencies, a cheap supervised route to test high-risk AI before the AI Act’s obligations apply.
