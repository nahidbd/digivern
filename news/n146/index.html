India’s central bank proposes a ’Stop Switch’ and an optional trusted-person approval to cut digital banking fraud

23 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · media sourcing

On Monday 23 November 2026 the Reserve Bank of India published seven sets of draft amendment directions on preventive safeguards for electronic banking transactions, covering commercial, small finance, payments, regional rural, local area and co-operative banks. Banks would have to offer a 'Stop Switch' that immediately blocks all future outward debits, new mandates and UPI registration on new third-party apps. Customers could also name a trusted person, aged 18 or over, who would have one hour to approve transfers above a threshold the customer sets; UPI, merchant and recurring payments are excluded. Digital channels would be off by default when an account is opened, and the definition of bank negligence would be widened. Comments are due by 14 December 2026. For commercial banks the safeguards would generally apply from 1 July 2027, and the negligence provisions from 1 January 2027.

Why it matters

It moves India's approach to payment fraud from compensating victims after the event towards customer-controlled limits and kill switches built into banking channels.
