Peru launches a free digital national ID card in its Perú Pass wallet, built on the open-source Inji platform

18–25 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · official sourcing

On 18 November 2026 Peru’s identity registry RENIEC approved the technical characteristics of the digital national identity document (DNId) and of the Perú Pass verifiable-credentials wallet by Resolución Jefatural N.º D000076-2026-RENIEC/JNAC, and began issuing the DNId nationwide. Adults with a valid physical DNI and a compatible smartphone can obtain it free of charge: they set a six-digit code, read the card’s machine-readable zone and pass fingerprint and face checks in the ID Perú app, and parents can request DNIds for their children. The digital card has the same legal validity as the physical one. Verification uses temporary QR codes, so photos or screenshots are not accepted, and each data transfer needs biometric approval or a PIN; a lost phone’s credential can be revoked on RENIEC’s website. Perú Pass is built on Inji, an open-source wallet, with advice from the Centre for Digital Public Infrastructure (CDPI). RENIEC says 99.3% of Peruvians already hold a national ID; a regulation on how the DNId may be used is still to be drafted.

Why it matters

Peru has put its national ID into a wallet built on open-source software, with the same legal force as the card, giving other countries a working template for verifiable digital credentials.
