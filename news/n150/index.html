World’s privacy regulators adopt 11 principles for AI tools in schools, including a model charter and limits on using pupils’ data

20–23 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · official sourcing

The 48th Global Privacy Assembly, the network of data protection authorities, has adopted a resolution on artificial intelligence in education, which France's CNIL presented on 20 November 2026 as chair of the Assembly's digital education working group. It sets 11 principles for public authorities, schools and education technology suppliers, covering the child's best interests, impact assessments, privacy by design and by default, retention, oversight of automated decisions, security, procurement and children's rights. Annexes provide a model charter for schools and guidance on AI literacy. ActuIA reported on 23 November that the text, agreed at a closed session in Brussels on 16–17 November, bars uses such as emotion recognition, social scoring and training commercial general-purpose models on pupils' data. The resolution is not binding.

Why it matters

It gives ministries and schools a shared international checklist for buying and using AI tools with children's data, even where national law is silent.
