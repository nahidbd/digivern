DR Congo moves court fees to a digital payment platform and links courts, prosecutors and prisons, with EU and UNDP support

5–6 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

DR Congo’s Minister of Justice, Guillaume Ngefa Atondoko Andali, launched in Kinshasa on 5 November 2026 the digitisation of judicial fee collection and the interconnection of courts, prosecutors’ offices and prisons, according to an official release published on allAfrica. The project is part of the 2025–2029 Joint Programme to Support Justice Reform, with technical support from UNDP and financial support from the European Union; Digital Economy Minister Augustin Kibassa Maliba, the EU ambassador and UNDP’s resident representative attended. A secure, traceable and verifiable payment platform is to replace manual collection of fees step by step. Ecofin Agency reports that judicial revenue is projected at CDF 207.4 billion (about $80.2 million) in 2026 under the revised finance law, and that the reform aims to cut overcharging and lost receipts.

Why it matters

Cash collection of court fees is a common route for overcharging; a traceable payment platform linked to case and prison records can raise recorded revenue and make abuses easier to spot.
