Bangladesh’s Tk 21,238 crore, 20-year spectrum renewal for mobile operators stalls; the regulator extends licences by a month

12–13 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

The government expects Tk 21,238 crore, excluding tax and VAT, from Grameenphone, Robi and Banglalink to renew their spectrum for 20 years, a structure the Finance Division approved on 8 November, The Business Standard reports. The renewal deadline passed on 10 November without agreement, and the Bangladesh Telecommunication Regulatory Commission (BTRC) extended the existing licences by one month. Operators object to the terms: 12 instalments over 10.5 years, simple interest of 8.11% from the third instalment if the balance is not cleared within a year, a 15% annual charge on missed instalments, and a no-dues certificate from the National Board of Revenue. Prices are Tk 316 crore per MHz in the 900 MHz band, Tk 264 crore in 1800 MHz and Tk 229 crore in 2100 MHz; Grameenphone holds 32.4 MHz, Robi 29.4 MHz and Banglalink 21 MHz. The BTRC’s 15 October proposal had been about Tk 13,100 crore for 12 years and four months. Citing GSMA, the paper notes that spectrum costs equal 15.7% of operators’ recurring revenue in Bangladesh, against 10.4% in Asia-Pacific and 7.7% globally. BTRC chairman Maj Gen (retd) Md Emdad Ul Bari said a separate auction of 8.4 MHz in the E-GSM band is planned. In a Daily Star column, consultant Mahtab Uddin Ahmed argued that any spectrum relief should be tied to published commitments on coverage, speed and price.

Why it matters

Spectrum costs shape how far and how cheaply mobile broadband reaches Bangladeshis, and with it the uptake of Bangla QR, mobile financial services and online government services.
