Bangladesh’s National Financial Inclusion Survey puts formal inclusion at 64.5%, but only 21% of mobile money users use apps

18 Nov 2026 · 4 sources · media sourcing

The Bangladesh National Financial Inclusion Survey 2025 was presented at an event at the InterContinental Dhaka on 18 November 2026, with M Abdur Razzaque, chairman of Research and Policy Integration for Development (RAPID), giving the keynote and Bangladesh Bank Deputy Governor Md Habibur Rahman as chief guest. It puts financial inclusion at 64.50%, leaving 35% of the population excluded (35.5% of adults, as the Dhaka Tribune reports it). Only 8% of bank account holders use debit cards and 21% of mobile financial services (MFS) account holders use mobile apps; bill payments and other services make up less than 4% of MFS use, as most users mainly send and receive money. About 34% of MFS users transact on basic phones costing Tk1,000 to Tk1,200, and 55.7% of women lack a personal mobile phone. bKash CEO Kamal Quadir said people without phones cannot use MFS and must travel to distant bank branches, while Bangladesh Bank Executive Director Rup Ratan Pine said women and marginalised groups must be brought into formal finance.

Why it matters

It gives Bangladesh a baseline showing that account numbers have grown faster than real use of digital payments, especially among women without phones.
