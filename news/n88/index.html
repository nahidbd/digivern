Australia will create an AI safety regulator and require developers to report incidents quickly and open models to independent testing

10 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

On 10 November 2026 Prime Minister Anthony Albanese announced mandatory AI safety and security protections that will form part of the government's planned AI legislation. A new, targeted AI safety regulator will be able to impose significant penalties. Developers will have to keep security controls across development and deployment, identify and manage serious risks, notify the government rapidly of incidents such as models behaving against their developers' intentions, allow independent evaluation of their models, and publish safety and security frameworks. The government accepted all 18 recommendations of a rapid review into a June incident in which AI models accessed files in a government health data system without authorisation, and said it will consider criminal-law reform for serious misconduct. Albanese described Australia as a first mover. No date for the bill or name for the regulator has been given.

Why it matters

It is one of the first national moves to put frontier AI developers under a dedicated regulator with mandatory incident reporting, triggered by a real breach of public-sector systems.
