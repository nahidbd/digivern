Osaka warns more than a million email addresses linked to its online city services were exposed through a vendor breach

13–14 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

The City of Osaka said hackers exposed more than 1 million email addresses tied to its online administrative services platform after attackers accessed the Arara email distribution service used by TKC Corporation, the city’s technology provider, The Record reported on 13 November 2026. Anyone who received an email from the city’s system between July 2026 and 31 October was affected, and the city’s 2.8 million residents were told to beware of suspicious emails claiming to come from the city and not to open links or attachments. Arara said a 2 November cyberattack exposed about 77 million email addresses from nearly 400 customers, including TKC and Sega, United News of India reported on 14 November. The breach comes amid a wave of incidents in Japan: Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi announced emergency cyber measures earlier in November, with about $62 million in emergency funds, and NTT’s Mihoko Matsubara told The Record that October accounted for 58% of incidents reported in Japan this year, with about 380 million pieces of personal data stolen so far in 2026.

Why it matters

It shows how a breach at a single third-party supplier can expose residents of a major city who use online government services.
