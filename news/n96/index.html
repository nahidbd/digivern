Germany’s Bundestag passes a law letting the BSI and federal police act against attackers’ systems, including deleting or altering data

12–13 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · media sourcing

The Bundestag passed the federal government’s bill to strengthen cybersecurity on 12 November 2026 with the votes of the governing CDU/CSU and SPD; the AfD, The Left and the Greens voted against. The law gives the Federal Office for Information Security (BSI), the Federal Police and the Federal Criminal Police Office (BKA) new powers to defend against cyberattacks: they may prohibit the operation of threatening IT systems, redirect data traffic and take down servers abroad, and, as a last resort, access attackers’ systems to read, delete or alter data, Reuters reported, adding that this is not a classic “hack-back”. Amendments let the BKA use the powers against physical dangers such as internet-controlled drones or explosives, and tightened proportionality rules and the protection of private IT. Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt said active cyber defence becomes a core part of Germany’s security architecture; Green MP Konstantin von Notz said the bill lacks clarity and creates redundant structures. The Bundesrat must still consent and has already asked for changes, mainly over the burden on businesses. dpa cited the industry association Bitkom’s estimate that cyberattacks cost the German economy €160.4–205.8 billion a year.

Why it matters

Germany is moving from defending its own networks to acting against attackers’ infrastructure; how proportionality and oversight work in practice will matter to other EU states weighing similar powers.
