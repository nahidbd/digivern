Central banks of four Central Asian states and Azerbaijan back a plan to link their national QR payment systems

23 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

Central bank representatives from Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, Azerbaijan, Kyrgyzstan and Tajikistan, meeting in Tajikistan on 22–23 October 2026, supported Kazakhstan’s proposed approach to linking their national QR payment systems, according to the National Bank of Kazakhstan, as reported by The Times of Central Asia on 23 November. Customers of participating banks would be able to pay merchants with their own banking apps when travelling in the other countries, with real-time cross-border transfers; the central banks aim to agree the mechanism by the end of the year, and no date has been set for customers to start using it. Uzbekistan’s HUMO and Kazakhstan’s National Payment Corporation already signed a memorandum in September 2026 on accepting each other’s national QR codes. Uzbek statistics show about 2.4 million visitors from Kazakhstan to Uzbekistan and about 1.1 million Uzbek trips to Kazakhstan in January–September 2026. Azerbaijan’s instant payment system settles only in manat, so payments from other currencies will need conversion.

Why it matters

Linking national QR schemes lets travellers and traders pay across borders with apps they already use, following the Asian model of connecting payment DPI rather than building a new regional system.
