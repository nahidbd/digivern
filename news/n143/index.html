Pakistan’s prime minister is due to launch the ‘Mehfooz Dawa’ app on 24 November so patients can scan medicine barcodes to spot fakes

22 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif is due to inaugurate the Mehfooz Dawa (‘safe medicine’) app in Islamabad on 24 November, ARY News reported on 22 November, citing sources. Consumers will scan the 2D barcode on a medicine pack to check that it is genuine and see its expiry date, usage instructions and retail price. The app sits on a mandatory track-and-trace system overseen by the Drug Regulatory Authority of Pakistan (DRAP) for human and veterinary medicines: products made from 9 October must carry a unique 2D barcode and serial number, and import and sale without one are banned from that date, while older batches are exempt. Earlier, on 9 November, Ministry of National Health Services spokesperson Sajid Shah urged the public to use the app, saying about 800 pharmaceutical companies and more than 90,000 medicines are registered with DRAP, Minute Mirror reported. The barcode plan was first proposed in 2015 and approved by the federal cabinet in 2017, but was delayed for years.

Why it matters

Serialised barcodes that anyone can check with a phone are one of the few practical tools against counterfeit medicines; how many pharmacies and patients actually scan will decide whether it works.
