India’s central bank gives Paytm’s payments arm in-principle approval to bring back its wallet

13–14 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · media sourcing

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has granted in-principle authorisation to Paytm Payments Services Ltd (PPSL), a wholly owned subsidiary of One 97 Communications, to issue prepaid payment instruments (PPIs), including wallets, the company said in an exchange filing reported by Business Standard on 13 November 2026. The RBI letter is dated 13 November; PPSL had applied on 31 December 2025. The approval is not final: according to NewsBytes, PPSL has six months to complete a systems audit, including a cybersecurity audit, and submit the report to the RBI, or the in-principle authorisation lapses automatically. Paytm’s wallet previously ran through Paytm Payments Bank, which the RBI barred from onboarding new customers in March 2022; Business Standard reports that the RBI cancelled that bank’s licence on 24 April 2026. Paytm’s group CFO Madhur Deora had earlier told analysts that a wallet “is an option” but not as sticky as before, and industry participants say general-purpose wallets have lost ground to UPI. One 97 Communications shares rose as much as 1.8% to ₹1,960 on 13 November.

Why it matters

It shows the RBI letting a major fintech back into wallets only through a separately licensed entity and under audit conditions, after the payments-bank failure.
