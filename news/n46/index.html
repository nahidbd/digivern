Ethiopia’s Fayda ID passes 52 million registrations as FaydaVerse studies blockchain credentials with a Korean firm

1–3 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

FaydaVerse, the state-owned company that now runs Ethiopia’s national ID programme under Ethiopian Investment Holdings, says more than 52 million people have registered for Fayda, with registration machines up from fewer than 100 to 15,000, and that 150 institutions are seeking to work with the programme. Identity verification fees of about 5 to 10 US cents per request began in August 2026; IDs stay free for citizens. On 26 October FaydaVerse signed an agreement with South Korea’s DSRV to set up a joint working group to study upgrades to the FaydaPass digital wallet and a proposed blockchain verification layer, FaydaChain, as well as a public-assistance e-voucher system that would verify beneficiaries through Fayda ID. These are studies, not deployments.

Why it matters

Ethiopia is turning a fast-growing ID into a paid verification and wallet business; how it prices verification and governs reusable credentials will matter for banks, social protection and other African countries considering the same model.
