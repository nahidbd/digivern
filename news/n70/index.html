Nigeria’s central bank sets licensing rules for stablecoin issuers, with capital of ₦10bn for naira coins and ₦15bn for foreign-currency coins

5–8 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · official sourcing

The Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) has issued Regulations for the Operations of Non-Security Virtual Assets in Nigeria, covering stablecoin issuers, deposit money tokens issued by banks and virtual asset service providers. Issuers of naira-referenced stablecoins (Class 1) need minimum paid-up capital of ₦10 billion and issuers of stablecoins referenced to foreign currencies (Class 2) ₦15 billion, and the CBN may set higher amounts; Class 2 issuers must also hold a minimum buffer of operational liquidity. The circular announcing the rules is dated 5 November 2026 and signed by Musa Jimoh, the CBN’s director of payments system policy, Vanguard reported; it says offshore issuers get no exemption from incorporation, licensing or capital requirements. Providers must apply enhanced due diligence and limits to customers who trade peer-to-peer as a business and report them to the CBN. Africa Fintech Network reports that the rules took effect on 5 November and that Class 2 stablecoins may be used for cross-border payments and remittances.

Why it matters

Stablecoins are already used for payments and remittances in Nigeria; the rules bring their issuers under central bank licensing and capital requirements instead of leaving them offshore, and other African regulators facing the same question now have a worked example.
