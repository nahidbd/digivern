UK financial regulator sets out good and poor practice for firms using AI, including agentic tools

19 Oct 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

The Financial Conduct Authority published examples of good practice and areas for improvement in how financial firms use machine learning, generative AI and agentic AI, drawn from its 2026 supervisory testing. It creates no new rules: existing rules, including the Consumer Duty and senior managers’ accountability, still apply. Good practice includes independent panels that challenge AI use cases and named senior owners for each one. Weak practice includes deploying AI before governance is in place and treating human review as a fix for flawed systems. The FCA also plans a framework for “agentic finance” covering agent identity and delegated authority.

Why it matters

Regulators elsewhere are watching how the FCA supervises AI under existing law rather than new AI-specific rules; the approach offers a template for central banks and financial regulators in emerging markets.
