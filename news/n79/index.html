Bihar starts selling subsidised fertiliser against a QR code generated from the farmer’s AgriStack Farmer ID

9 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

The Bihar government is preparing to connect all fertiliser sales centres in the state to the AgriStack platform, Patna Press reports. Farmers would book fertiliser in advance with their Farmer ID; land records and crop details would be checked through a mobile app, which then generates a QR code on the farmer’s phone. At primary agricultural credit societies (PACS) or authorised retailers the code would be scanned on an e-POS device, and fertiliser handed over only after successful verification, while officials track stock in real time from PACS warehouses to retail centres. The state’s Cooperation Department says the aim is to curb black marketing, hoarding and smuggling of subsidised fertiliser. First Bihar reports that the process has begun in the Tirhut division of north Bihar, where special cooperative sales centres are being opened through PACS; no statewide start date or numbers were given. The state also plans to give PACS wholesale fertiliser licences and turn some into drone hire centres.

Why it matters

It is an early example of a state using the national Farmer ID to target an input subsidy at the point of sale, which is the main promised payoff of India’s 110 million Farmer IDs.
