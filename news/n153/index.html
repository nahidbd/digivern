Bangladesh briefs diplomats and donors on its draft cyber law amendment and gives them one week to send written recommendations

24 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

On 24 November 2026 Bangladesh’s foreign ministry in Dhaka hosted a consultation with heads of diplomatic missions and development partners on the draft Cyber Security (Amendment) Act, 2026. Posts, Telecommunications and IT Minister Faqir Mahbub Anam chaired the meeting, Law Minister Md Asaduzzaman was chief guest, and the director general of the National Cyber Security Agency, Dr Md Taiyebur Rahman, presented the draft. Representatives of the UK, the EU, Australia, Switzerland and UN agencies welcomed the proposed requirement for a court’s prior permission before journalists are arrested, but asked for clearer rules on rumours, misinformation and defamation, non-criminal remedies for defamation, tribunal approval for content blocking and a more precise definition of cyberspace. Diplomats also urged the government to involve civil society, and partners have one week to submit written recommendations. The law minister said the government wants to remove ambiguity before the bill goes to parliament, and further consultations with civil society and business are planned.

Why it matters

The final shape of Bangladesh’s cyber law will decide how far online speech remains criminalised and how content blocking is controlled, which matters for trust in the country’s wider digital agenda.
