Kenyan MPs tell the central bank to suspend new rules for digital lenders and other non-deposit-taking credit providers

12–13 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Kenya’s National Assembly Committee on Delegated Legislation on 12 November 2026 directed the Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) to suspend the rollout of its Non-Deposit-Taking Credit Providers Regulations and to work with parliamentary legal counsel to redraft the disputed provisions. The regulations (Legal Notice No. 191, published 29 September 2026) replace the Digital Credit Providers Regulations of 2022 and extend CBK oversight to digital and mobile loan apps, buy-now-pay-later, asset financing, pay-as-you-go and qualifying peer-to-peer lending. Providers with initial capital of Ksh 20 million or more would need a CBK licence and smaller ones would register; existing providers have until 29 March 2027 to apply. The rules ban threats, abusive language and the use of borrowers’ phone contacts to shame them, and set fines of up to Ksh 2 million or three times the gain made or loss avoided, plus daily penalties. MP Samuel Chepkonga said the rules give the CBK excessive discretion, and the committee said the Ksh 2 million penalty exceeds what the Statutory Instruments Act allows. Pulse Kenya notes that the amended CBK Act gives the bank an express power to impose penalties and that no formal annulment of the regulations had been reported.

Why it matters

Kenya was among the first countries to regulate app-based lenders after complaints about debt-shaming; the dispute between Parliament and the central bank decides how soon wider licensing and stronger borrower protections take effect.
