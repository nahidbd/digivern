Bangladesh seeks Estonia’s help to build its ‘One Citizen One ID’ system and a national interoperability framework

18 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Bangladesh’s minister for posts, telecommunications and ICT, Faqir Mahbub Anam, met Estonia’s non-resident ambassador to Bangladesh, Kristi Karelsohn, at the ICT Division in Dhaka on 18 November 2026. They discussed digital public infrastructure, the government’s ‘One Citizen One ID’ plan for a single digital identity for every citizen, and a national framework to let government systems exchange data. The minister said the work is backed by an EU-funded project involving Estonia’s e-Governance Academy and the British Council, and that the government wants the unified ID running as soon as possible. ICT Division Secretary Md Mamunur Rashid Bhuiyan said the project will support the interoperability framework, with data governance, personal data protection and cyber security laws as its legal basis. According to The Business Standard, a national data governance authority is being set up under the Prime Minister’s Office. Neither report gives a budget or timetable.

Why it matters

A single citizen ID and an interoperability layer would be the backbone of Bangladesh’s digital public infrastructure, and Estonia’s model shapes how it may be designed.
