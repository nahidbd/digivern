Ghana starts enforcing compulsory biometric checks of the Ghana Card, with fines for institutions that do not comply

2–4 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · media sourcing

Ghana’s National Identification Authority (NIA) began a nationwide enforcement exercise on 3 November 2026 under L.I. 2523, the National Identity Register (Amendment) Regulations, 2026, which took effect on 9 June. Institutions may no longer verify customers by looking at, photocopying or scanning the Ghana Card. They must use approved biometric verification against the national identity database, through the NIA’s Identity Verification System Platform. A Biometric Verification Implementation Task Force will monitor compliance. Breaches carry fines of 500 to 2,000 penalty units, which Biometric Update puts at GH¢6,000 to GH¢24,000 for companies and GH¢600 to GH¢6,000 for individuals; the NIA says it cannot impose fines itself and will take offenders to court, and offenders can be removed from the verification platform. Telecom operators may keep using match-on-card fingerprint checks, citizens do not need to re-enrol, and an NIA app for small businesses has been built but not yet launched.

Why it matters

Mandatory biometric checks against the national register are meant to cut identity fraud in banking and other services, but they raise questions about access for small businesses and people whose biometric checks fail.
