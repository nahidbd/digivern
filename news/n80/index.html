Canada launches its Real-Time Rail, a 24/7 instant payment system with mandatory central fraud checks, about seven years after its first target

9 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

Payments Canada launched the Real-Time Rail (RTR) on 9 November 2026: a system that clears and settles payments instantly, 24 hours a day, every day of the year, using the ISO 20022 message standard. Payments Canada owns and runs it, and the Bank of Canada oversees it. Eight participants are live at launch: Wealthsimple, Questbank, Peoples Trust Company, Paramount Commerce, KOHO, DoBusiness.com, Digital Commerce Bank and Bank of America; all major Canadian financial institutions are to join in the coming months, and Payments Canada expects the RTR to reach more than nine in ten Canadians within its first year. Payments Canada says Canada is the first G7 country to launch a real-time system with mandatory centralised fraud services: confirmation of payee (for consumer accounts from day one), a fraud transaction safety score, a shared list of fraud risks and a fraud reporting platform. Interac is the technical operator and first fraud services provider, and Interac e-Transfer payments are starting to clear and settle on the RTR; CGI, Deloitte Canada and IBM Canada were delivery partners. Its published timeline moves further Interac e-Transfer participants onto the RTR in the first half of 2027, with all launch-phase participants at full volumes by the third quarter of 2027. The Canadian Press notes the system was first meant to be in place by 2019. Payments Canada cites a C.D. Howe Institute estimate of up to C$14.5 billion in cost savings.

Why it matters

Canada joins Brazil, India and the euro area with a national instant payment rail, and makes fraud controls such as confirmation of payee compulsory from day one rather than adding them after losses, a design choice other central banks are weighing.
