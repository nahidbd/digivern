Uganda Cancer Institute pilots a Korean handheld AI device to support cervical cancer screening

10–11 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

The Uganda Cancer Institute (UCI) is piloting CerviCARE, a handheld cervical cancer screening device that uses artificial intelligence and was developed in South Korea. The pilot is part of a joint project between UCI and the Korea Foundation for International Healthcare (KOFIH), and results were shared with Ministry of Health officials at a meeting in Kampala on 10 November 2026. The wider programme, the three-year National Cancer Management and Capacity Building Project, is backed by about US$3 million under a 2023 agreement; by February 2026 it had screened more than 35,000 women aged 25–65 in Kampala City, Mbarara City and Mbarara District and treated over 3,000 with pre-cancerous lesions. The AI tool supports visual inspection with acetic acid, the low-cost screening method used in many public clinics.

Why it matters

Point-of-care AI that improves a cheap screening method could extend early detection in clinics with no specialists, if accuracy holds up in local use.
