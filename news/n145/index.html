Malaysia’s Health Ministry reports unauthorised access to a file-sharing platform at one facility; patient services unaffected

12–14 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · media sourcing

Malaysia’s Ministry of Health (MOH) said on 14 November that a cyber incident detected on 12 November gave unauthorised access to a supporting file storage and sharing platform at one of its facilities, Bernama and the New Straits Times reported. A preliminary investigation by the ministry’s Computer Security Incident Response Team found no encryption of its main databases, and major operational systems and patient services were not affected. Public access to the platform was blocked, passwords were reset, servers audited and multi-factor authentication enforced. The ministry said it would work with the National Cyber Security Agency (NACSA) under the Cyber Security Act 2024 and warned that possessing or spreading unlawfully obtained government documents is a serious offence. The New Straits Times noted that the government had raised the national cyber threat level from low to moderate on 4 November.

Why it matters

Health ministries hold some of the most sensitive data in government; the incident is an early test of how Malaysia’s Cyber Security Act 2024 works for public health systems.
