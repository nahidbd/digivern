Chile delays its new personal data protection law to 15 October 2027 and enlarges the board of the future data protection agency

23 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · expert sourcing

Chile published Law 21.855 in the Diario Oficial on 23 November 2026, pushing back the entry into force of its new personal data protection law, Law 21.719, from 1 December 2026 to 15 October 2027. The Chamber of Deputies approved the bill on 17 November in the form passed by the Senate, and it was promulgated on 19 November; the government had asked for a longer delay to December 2027. The law enlarges the governing council of the new Personal Data Protection Agency from three to five members, with six-year non-renewable terms, and requires the Senate to approve the first five councillors as a single package by a two-thirds vote. Because of the short timetable, the President must put forward the first councillors within ten days of publication, and appointments must be made by 15 January 2027. A proposal to let large companies receive written warnings instead of fines during the first year was rejected, so only small firms keep that option; fines for very serious breaches reach 20,000 UTM, or up to 4% of annual revenue for repeat offenders.

Why it matters

The delay gives Chile's public bodies and companies more time, but puts pressure on the government to set up an independent regulator before one of Latin America's strictest GDPR-style laws takes effect.
