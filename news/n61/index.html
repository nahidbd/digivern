Uganda tells Parliament the EAC is building a regional payment system to cut reliance on the US dollar

3–4 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

In a statement to Uganda’s Parliament on 3 November 2026, the Minister for East African Community (EAC) Affairs said the EAC is developing an integrated payment and settlement system to reduce member states’ dependence on the US dollar in cross-border trade. Today’s East African Payment System (EAPS) links the national RTGS systems of Uganda, Kenya, Tanzania and Rwanda, so banks can settle in the Uganda shilling, Kenya shilling, Tanzania shilling and Rwanda franc. Burundi, South Sudan, the DR Congo and Somalia are not yet fully integrated. The aim is to let people and businesses send money between any two partner states through bank accounts, mobile money wallets or other authorised digital channels, under a Cross-Border Payment System Masterplan the EAC adopted in 2025. The masterplan also covers harmonising the licensing of payment service providers. The opposition’s shadow minister welcomed the plan but called for a central bank-backed liquidity facility, saying traders still go through dollars because banks lack regional currencies. No dates or budget were given.

Why it matters

Cheaper regional payments in local currencies, including mobile money, would cut costs for East African traders who now convert twice through the dollar.
