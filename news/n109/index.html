COP31 partners urge AI firms to disclose energy, carbon and water footprints and protect climate information

13 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

The Global Initiative for Information Integrity on Climate Change, co-chaired by the United Nations, UNESCO and Brazil, launched “Priorities for Action on AI and Climate Information Integrity” at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Türkiye, on 13 November 2026, UN News reported. The document calls on AI companies to assess how their systems affect the integrity of climate information and publish the findings, and to disclose their energy use and carbon, water and land footprints. It urges states to build information integrity into national AI strategies and climate plans and, according to WION, to require AI providers to disclose environmental impacts; it also asks developers to show sources and uncertainty and not to “reward agreement above accuracy”. Germany committed €2 million to the UNESCO-administered Global Fund for Information Integrity on Climate Change (more than $2.2 million, per UN News). A Charter for Climate Information Integrity in Advertising was launched the same day. Charlotte Scaddan of the UN Department of Global Communications said “The question isn’t simply whether AI is good or bad for the climate.”

Why it matters

It adds a UN-backed benchmark that governments can use when writing AI strategies, linking AI transparency on misinformation with disclosure of its environmental footprint.
