Abu Dhabi starts a “Machine-Readable Government” programme so AI can check permits against the rules

2 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Abu Dhabi signed four agreements with OpenAI on 2 November, after Crown Prince Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed met OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman. The main public-sector one is a Machine-Readable Government initiative led by the Department of Government Enablement: selected regulations are converted into a machine-readable form so that AI can help officials with compliance checks such as permits, while officials keep the final decision and every assessment can be traced back to the rule it relies on. The first pilot, already running, is with the Abu Dhabi Agriculture and Food Safety Authority on food imports, using models hosted on inference infrastructure in the UAE. The other agreements cover a five-year partnership with Inception42 on AI in government, business and education, investment analysis at Mubadala, and deployment at ADNOC. No values were disclosed.

Why it matters

Turning rules into code that AI can apply, with a human sign-off and an audit trail to the legal text, is one of the more concrete models for AI in public administration, and it starts with food-import permits.
