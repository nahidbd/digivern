ASEAN signs its first binding digital economy agreement, and ADB plans up to $5 billion to help put it into practice

14–16 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

ASEAN member states signed the Digital Economy Framework Agreement (DEFA) in Manila on 14 November 2026, the bloc’s first standalone, legally binding digital economy agreement, Biometric Update reported. It covers cross-border data flows, digital trade, digital payments, cybersecurity, artificial intelligence and source-code protection, and is to enter into force 180 days after signing, subject to ratification. Business digital identity may be an early priority: Singapore’s Deputy Prime Minister Gan Kim Yong said businesses “need to know who you are trading with”, and Vietnam is linking its VNeID with Singapore’s Singpass and Laos’ LAeID. The same day the Asian Development Bank (ADB) announced an ASEAN Regional Platform for AI and Digital Economy that will mobilise up to $5 billion in public and private financing by 2030 to implement DEFA, covering digital infrastructure, cross-border payments and verifiable IDs, cybersecurity and AI readiness. The platform completes ADB’s $30 billion pledge to ASEAN.

Why it matters

A binding regional rulebook backed by dedicated financing could make payments, digital IDs and data flows work across Southeast Asian borders, something South Asia has no equivalent for.
