Jordan signs a $4.2m World Bank grant to test AI in government services and open its Sanad digital ID app to businesses

31 Oct – 5 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Jordan’s Minister of Planning and International Cooperation, Zeina Toukan, and the World Bank’s Regional Director Dalia Khalifa signed a US$4.2 million grant agreement on 31 October 2026. It is additional financing for the Youth, Technology and Jobs Project, which the World Bank approved in March 2020 with US$200 million (a US$163.1 million IBRD loan and a US$36.9 million Global Concessional Financing Facility grant). The grant will pay for developing and testing AI applications in selected government sectors, AI tools delivered through Sanad (the government app that gives access to services with a digital identity), a plan to open Sanad gradually to business users, the redesign of targeted service procedures and outreach to international technology investors. Results of the AI pilots are to shape a planned national centre of excellence in AI. The Ministry of Digital Economy and Entrepreneurship will implement it through the project’s existing unit. According to figures reported by Jordan News, by June 2026 the project had digitised 178 government services against a target of 100, and about 52.9 million transactions had passed through digital government services.

Why it matters

Opening Sanad to businesses would make the national digital ID the key to company services as well, and the pilots are meant to show what a national AI centre should do before it is built.
