Greece expands its free public Digital Tutoring service to 50 exam subjects and plans AI practice tools

9–10 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · official sourcing

Greece’s Ministry of Education, Religious Affairs and Sports expanded its free Public Digital Tutoring service (Psifiako Frontistirio), which helps pupils prepare for the national university entrance exams, from 9 November 2026. The teaching staff rises from 80 to 89, coverage from 45 to 50 nationally examined subjects and live teaching from 477 to 489 hours a month. Vocational lyceum subjects rise from 19 to 21 and special-education subjects from four to seven; live lessons are interpreted into Greek Sign Language and recordings are posted with subtitles. Live classes are aimed at final-year lyceum pupils, while pupils from the fifth year of primary school upward get self-study material. The ministry says about 500,000 people had used the service by 31 October 2026 and more than 3,100 recorded lessons are available. The next phase will add EduAI, to tailor practice exercises and spot gaps, and an AI tutor for the foreign-language exams, donated by Microsoft.

Why it matters

A free state online tutoring service with about 500,000 users shows how a government can use digital tools to reduce families’ reliance on private exam coaching.
