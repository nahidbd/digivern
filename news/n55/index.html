Ghana’s national health information systems go offline over an unpaid US$64,510 cloud bill; facilities return to paper

29 Oct – 5 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · media sourcing

The Ghana Health Service’s electronic health information systems have been unavailable nationwide since 29 October 2026, after its cloud hosting provider, which 3News names as Akamai, shut down all 10 servers over what GHS called outstanding administrative issues. Affected systems include the District Health Information Management System (DHIMS2), the Planning and Budget Management Information System, the National Malaria Data Repository, HIV and TB trackers, SMS alerts and the birth-notification gateway to the Births and Deaths Registry and the National Identification Authority. In a memo dated 2 November, Director-General Dr Samuel Kaba Akoriyea told facilities and districts to keep paper registers and monthly summary forms for October and November so the data can be entered later; Android tracker apps keep capturing data offline. He said there was no security breach and no data was lost, because nightly encrypted backups are held outside the suspended account. GHS is seeking approval to pay US$64,510.95 to restore the account; rebuilding on new servers instead could take up to six weeks after capacity is procured. He said any later move to a Ghana-based provider would be a controlled project with a parallel run and a funded backup arrangement. 3News notes a 2023 review recording that the donor projects paying for the DHIMS2 servers had ended.

Why it matters

A national health data system was switched off over a bill of about US$65,000: health information systems and other DPI need recurrent hosting budgets and exit plans, not only donor-funded set-up costs.
