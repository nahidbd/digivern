Somalia moves aviation licensing online with SOFIA, a platform aligned with East African safety oversight rules

26–27 Nov 2026 · 1 source · media sourcing

Somalia’s civil aviation authority has launched the first phase of SOFIA, a digital platform that replaces paper-based procedures and lets airlines, pilots and other aviation professionals apply online, Dawan Africa reported from Mogadishu on 27 November. Personal licensing, including licence printing and medical checks, is now live, Director General Ahmed Moallim Hassan said; implementation has passed 50%, and modules for airworthiness, flight operations and aviation security will be completed on the next visit of experts from CASSOA, the East African Community’s Civil Aviation Safety and Security Oversight Agency. The platform follows CASSOA rules, and Somalia has aligned its aviation law with CASSOA regulations. The government has spent at least $500,000 on servers and other equipment and built a computerised examination room with cameras and access control. Airlines will receive certificates and documents automatically, and Hassan said he hoped Somalia would soon pass an audit by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO). “Everything is done on this software, and the meetings and delays are being eliminated,” he said.

Why it matters

Digital licensing that follows regional rules is part of how Somalia, the newest member of the East African Community, is rebuilding regulatory institutions and preparing for international safety audits.
