COP31 Presidency and ITU launch the Antalya AI Pledge to curb artificial intelligence’s climate footprint

16 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

The COP31 Presidency, held by Türkiye, and the International Telecommunication Union (ITU) launched the Antalya AI Pledge at the UN climate conference in Antalya on 16 November 2026, with an initial coalition of governments, companies, researchers and civil society, ITU said. The voluntary pledge has four pillars: sustainable AI infrastructure, with low- and zero-emission energy and efficient use of energy and water in data centres; a responsible AI lifecycle, including circular approaches to e-waste; standardised reporting of AI’s environmental footprint, supported by ITU’s L.1801 (2026) standard; and finance and technical support for AI-for-climate work in developing countries, least developed countries and small island developing states. Progress is to be tracked every year at the UN climate conference until 2030. UN News reported that by the morning of 16 November the pledge had 80 endorsements and 271 commitments, and quoted ITU Deputy Secretary-General Tomas Lamanauskas warning that data-centre energy use is expected to double by 2030. ITU Secretary-General Doreen Bogdan-Martin said AI “must be used responsibly”.

Why it matters

It gives governments and AI companies a shared, if voluntary, yardstick for reporting the energy, water and e-waste costs of AI, which most national AI strategies leave out.
