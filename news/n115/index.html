Costa Rica makes its smartphone digital ID compulsory to accept in public and private institutions from 1 January 2027

10–16 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

Costa Rica’s Supreme Electoral Tribunal (TSE), which keeps the civil registry, announced on 10 November 2026 that from 1 January 2027 all public and private institutions must accept the Identidad Digital Costarricense (IDC), an identity card held on a mobile phone, which has the same legal validity as the physical card. Biometric Update reported on 16 November that institutions may not demand the physical card as well, and that the requirement follows a TSE ruling of February 2026 which rejected an exemption for the police and gave the judiciary until 1 January 2027 to comply. The IDC is activated through the IDC-Ciudadano app and protected by a PIN and facial recognition; it costs ₡2,600, is valid for four years and is free for older adults. The TSE says 107,243 people requested it between its launch on 9 September 2025 and 1 November 2026. The physical identity card remains free.

Why it matters

Compulsory acceptance is what turns a digital ID from an optional app into usable public infrastructure; with about 107,000 users so far, the January deadline will test both institutions and take-up.
