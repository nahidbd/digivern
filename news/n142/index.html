Singapore sets a 2027–2028 roadmap for PayNow, including interoperable QR codes and higher limits for government payments

18 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · official sourcing

The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) and the Association of Banks in Singapore published an implementation roadmap on 18 November for the next phase of PayNow, the city-state’s instant payment service, based on the Phase 2 report of the PayNow Generation 2 study. MAS will lead the work over 2027 and 2028 with the Singapore Payments Network (SPaN) and Banking Computer Services. Planned changes include an interoperable SGQR+ code, with a limited rollout in 2027 and expansion in 2028, so people can pay any merchant with their preferred app and Singapore can connect to more regional QR networks. Common deep-linking standards to move shoppers from online checkouts to their payment app are due by end-2027. Higher PayNow limits for government payments with instant confirmation will start with one agency in late 2027. SPaN will set rules on disputes, refunds, fraud and scams during 2028, and richer payment data is targeted from end-2027. MAS also said it will prepare for payments made by AI agents and develop a Future Payment Rails Blueprint in 2027.

Why it matters

It shows how a mature instant payment system plans for QR interoperability, consumer protection and AI-agent payments, issues Bangladesh and others now face.
