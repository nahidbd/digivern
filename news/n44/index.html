Greece launches the myEOPYY app for health claims and lets patients rate the doctors they visit

3 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Greece’s national health insurer EOPYY launched the myEOPYY app on 3 November, presented by Health Minister Adonis Georgiadis. The app puts each insured person’s health insurance record on their phone (cover, doctor visits, procedures, hospital stays, prescriptions and refunds), lets users photograph and upload receipts and track reimbursement claims in real time, including for children and dependants, issues an electronic ticket for collecting high-cost medicines at EOPYY pharmacies and shows average waiting times. A new provider evaluation system sends an SMS after a visit with a short questionnaire; the first phase covers contracted private specialists, diagnostic laboratories and physiotherapy centres. Ministry figures put 4.6 million social security numbers (AMKA) linked to the health insurance record, and 77% of claims in the past month arrived digitally.

Why it matters

It turns a payer’s back-office record into a citizen-facing service and adds structured patient feedback on providers, a feedback loop that most national digital health programmes still lack.
