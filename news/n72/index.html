USDA adds an AI check to its crop production reports after errors in its September corn data

6–7 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

The US Department of Agriculture will use an artificial intelligence tool it built to check its crop production report due on 10 November, Reuters reported on 6 November 2026. Officials found that the tool would have flagged the errors in the department’s September corn data. Lance Honig, who chairs the statistics board of the National Agricultural Statistics Service (NASS), said a computer file had wrongly combined data on irrigated and non-irrigated corn acreage; the correction raised the yield estimate by about 3% in Nebraska and lowered it by about 2% in Kansas. Under Secretary Scott Hutchins said the tool adds to the existing validation rather than replacing it, and that the errors were not caused by staff losses. The tool is part of a pilot that relies more on technology for acreage and yield estimates as fewer farmers answer surveys.

Why it matters

Official crop statistics move markets and guide food policy; as survey response falls, statistical offices are turning to AI and satellite data, which makes transparent checks on those tools part of the job.
