Attack through a refrigeration contractor hits cold-room controls at France’s national blood service

2–4 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

A cybersecurity incident on Monday 2 November hit the systems that monitor and control cold rooms at regional sites of the Établissement français du sang (EFS), France’s national blood service; AFP reports three sites were affected. The attack came through a refrigeration contractor, which Le Canard Enchaîné named as Coolworld, and changed the operating settings of cold rooms. On 4 November the office of Health Minister Stéphanie Rist and the EFS said blood products were safe and none had been lost, because independent alarms raised the alert. The EFS cut remote access to the equipment, moved stocks to other rooms and increased monitoring; deputy managing director Sophie Le Cam said further layers of security would be added. Coolworld said a limited number of units were affected through remote-monitoring software and a third-party component. The attacker and method have not been identified.

Why it matters

This was an attack on equipment controls rather than data, reached through a supplier, at a piece of critical health infrastructure; it shows why health cyber rules need to cover contractors and operational technology, not only hospital IT.
