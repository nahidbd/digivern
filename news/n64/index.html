Nigeria asks the World Bank for a third extension of its $430m digital ID project as 59 million people still lack a NIN

5 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Nigeria has asked the World Bank to extend its Digital Identification for Development (ID4D) project by 12 months, moving the closing date from 31 December 2026 to 31 December 2027, according to a World Bank restructuring paper dated 22 October 2026. This would be the project’s third extension. The project is co-financed by the World Bank’s IDA, the French Development Agency and the European Investment Bank, with $430 million in total, of which about $196 million (roughly 46%) is undisbursed. About 121 million people had a National Identification Number (NIN) by September 2026, against a target of 180 million. Enrolment runs at about 1.5 million a month, against the 3.9 million needed. The contract for the new MOSIP-based National Identity Management System 2.0 was signed on 15 September 2026, with full deployment and data migration due by 15 December 2027. Enrolment partners have not been paid since January 2026, and coverage of women and girls (40.4 million against 79 million) and of children under 16 (26.3 million against 60.83 million) is well behind target.

Why it matters

Delays in Africa’s largest digital ID programme hold back everything that depends on the NIN, from bank KYC to social transfers.
