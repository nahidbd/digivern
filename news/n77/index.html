India’s central bank consolidates cyber security rules for non-bank payment operators, with six-hour incident reporting and a 12-hour cooling period after a phone or email change

5–9 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · official sourcing

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has issued the Reserve Bank of India (Cyber Resilience and Digital Payment Security Controls for non-bank PSOs) Master Directions, 2026, dated 5 November 2026. They apply to all authorised non-bank payment system operators, grouped as large (including NPCI, card networks, payment aggregators, large prepaid wallet issuers, ATM networks and TReDS platforms), medium and small. The directions take effect immediately for large and medium operators and from 1 April 2028 for small ones. Operators must report cyber attacks, outages of critical systems, internal fraud and settlement delays to the RBI within six hours of detection, and cyber incidents to CERT-In; impose a cooling period of at least 12 hours before online payments when a customer’s registered mobile number or email changes; use multi-factor authentication for electronic debits; run real-time fraud monitoring with a 24x7 manned desk for fraud reports; keep audit logs for at least five years; and submit an annual system audit, with each finding fixed within three months. The directions consolidate the July 2024 rules with older circulars on ATM switch providers and system audits, MediaNama reports.

Why it matters

Non-bank operators such as NPCI, wallets and payment aggregators carry most of India’s digital payments; one rulebook with fixed reporting times and customer safeguards is a reference for other central banks regulating fintech-led payment systems.
