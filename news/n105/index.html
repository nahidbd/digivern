Bangladesh publishes a revised draft amendment to its cyber law, cutting penalties for “rumours” and defamation but keeping them criminal

11–15 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Bangladesh’s ICT Division has posted a revised draft of the Cyber Security (Amendment) Act 2026 for public comment until 25 November 2026, Prothom Alo reported on 13 November. The first draft, published in September, proposed a new section 26A punishing the spread of “rumours” and “disinformation” with up to 10 years in prison and/or a fine of Tk 40 lakh, and widened section 25 to cover defamation, humiliation and bullying, including AI-generated content. The revised text cuts section 26A to up to five years and Tk 10 lakh and defines a rumour as deliberately false or distorted information; defamation carries up to two years and Tk 5 lakh, with the definition taken from section 499 of the Penal Code. A new section 46(a) requires tribunal approval before arrests over content published by registered media, and outlets must receive 15 days’ written notice before action is taken against them. Blocking requests are to go through the Director General of the National Cyber Security Agency, with reasons given within three days and restoration decided within 15 days; arrest without warrant is kept for attacks on critical infrastructure. In The Daily Star on 15 November, UNESCO’s representative in Bangladesh, Susan Vize, and Miraj Ahmed Chowdhury of Digitally Right urged the government to remove the new speech offences and hold time-bound consultations.

Why it matters

The amendment decides whether Bangladesh’s cyber law again criminalises online speech, as the Digital Security Act 2018 and Cyber Security Act 2023 did; the comment period to 25 November is the opportunity for citizens, media and industry to weigh in.
