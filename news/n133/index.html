Uganda makes banks verify customers online against the national ID database, but only 10 of 107 partners are connected

18 Nov 2026 · 2 sources · media sourcing

Uganda’s Internal Affairs Minister Ephraim Kamuntu told Parliament on 18 November 2026 that banks and other financial institutions must integrate with the National Identification and Registration Authority’s (NIRA) new National Security Information System (NSIS). Under it, customer details are retrieved from the NIRA database only when an institution presents a National Identification Number, rather than relying on details read from the physical card, which the minister said strengthens security, privacy and data protection. NIRA has shared integration guidelines with 107 partners, but only 10 were transacting on the system by 28 October 2026, and the Uganda Bankers’ Association says many banks are still installing compatible biometric readers. Where a card cannot be verified, customers can be checked through NIRA’s secure channel or must obtain a Confirmation of Information Letter. MPs Paul Mwiru and Denes Sekabira said this adds costs and delays for citizens, while Deputy Speaker Thomas Tayebwa backed the measure as a defence against forged IDs used for money laundering and terrorist financing.

Why it matters

It shows the practical cost of moving ID checks in financial services from cards to online verification, a step many countries with national ID systems are now taking.
