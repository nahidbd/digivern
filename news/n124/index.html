Nairobi says 99% of its public health facilities are now digitised and linked to SHA, helping lift facility revenue to KSh2.6 billion

18 Nov 2026 · 3 sources · media sourcing

Nairobi City County says its health facilities have increased annual revenue from about KSh800 million to KSh2.6 billion, crediting Social Health Authority (SHA) reimbursements, better claims management and the digitisation of hospital operations. Governor Johnson Sakaja gave the figures at the Third Annual Nairobi Health Scientific Conference and said the county targets KSh4 billion this financial year. Health CEC Suzanne Silantoi said 99 per cent of county health facilities have been digitised and integrated into the SHA system, letting them submit claims and receive reimbursements. Sakaja said more than three million Nairobi residents have registered under SHA, and the county is also using AI-assisted digital X-ray screening for tuberculosis and digital community health systems. Individual facilities such as Westlands Health Centre went from about KSh200,000 to KSh6 million a month.

Why it matters

It is a concrete county-level case of digital claims systems changing how public hospitals are financed under Kenya’s new health insurance scheme.
